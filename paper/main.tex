\begin{abstract}
\noindent\textbf{Headline.} We determine the exact minimum state
product $\Mn$ for $n \in \{3, \ldots, 9\}$ in Dijkstra's
central-daemon model, unconditional at $n \in \{3, 4\}$, and
conditional for $n \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$ on the replayable
lower-bound certificates of \cref{app:exhaust}, and conjecture
$\Mn = 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ for all $n \ge 9$.  Between $n = 8$ and
$n = 9$ the ratio $\Mn / M_{n-1}$ breaks from $3$ to $27/8$: a
small-$n$ absorber family saturates the bound through $n = 8$, the
ternary-strip family saturates at $n = 9$, and no witness from
either family extends past its crossover point
(\cref{obs:ratio-crossover,thm:non-extension}).

\medskip\noindent\textbf{Evidence.} The upper bound is realized
through $n = 10$ by a Lean 4 / Mathlib formalization of the
ternary-strip witness CUP-2, with a Python-verified sibling
construction (CLB) extending
through $n = 18$.  The lower bound is backed through $n = 9$ by
the replayable exhaustive-search procedure of \cref{app:exhaust},
with worked-example certificates shipped at small $n$ and the
full per-orientation stream regenerable via the shipped driver.  Structural
detectors on a $97$-record stratified corpus separate valid
witnesses from sub-threshold candidates: a circulation linear
program on the lifted-defect graph separates $93$ of $97$ records,
and a strict upgrade, the \emph{sink kernel} of the forced-move
digraph on non-good configurations (Axis C), a structural
certificate that rules out valid rule-table completions of
candidate cycles, separates all $97$.  Sink-kernel nonemptiness
on every tested sub-threshold record combined with a general
monotonicity lemma in $\detOf$ yields a one-sided sub-threshold
certificate on those records.  Under the relaxed-connectedness
convention of the 1985 Stanford seminar~\cite{HaddadKnuth1985}, an
SMT encoding newly certifies $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_5 = 96$
(\cref{sec:main-relaxed}), the first case at $n \ge 5$ in which
$M_n^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_n$.

\medskip\noindent\textbf{What remains.} A uniform-in-$n$
analytical lower bound is open; the mechanism of the ratio
crossover at $n = 9$ is open (the 1985 ARG LCM bound is ruled
out on both scope and insensitivity grounds; see
\cref{sec:lower-arg}; the handle ``ARG'' comes from the unsigned
Stanford seminar notes~\cite[\S2--\S3]{HaddadKnuth1985});
transport of the large-$n$ bounds to the relaxed-connectedness
convention is open beyond $n = 5$.  A systematic catalog of failed
analytical routes (\cref{sec:landscape}) locates the obstruction at
the joint interaction of the good cycle, its mover sequence, and
the determined rule-table entries.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}\label{sec:intro}

\subsection{Dijkstra's self-stabilization and the product-minimization question}\label{sec:intro-dijkstra}

In 1974 Dijkstra~\cite{Dijkstra1974} posed a now-canonical problem in
distributed computing: design $n$ ring processors, each with only
local visibility of its two neighbors, whose joint dynamics under a
non-deterministic central daemon converges to mutual exclusion from
\emph{any} initial configuration.  A processor $P_i$ has $m_i$ states
and a local transition function
$f_i : \mathrm{Fin}(m_{i-1}) \times \mathrm{Fin}(m_i) \times \mathrm{Fin}(m_{i+1}) \to \mathrm{Fin}(m_i)$.
$P_i$ is \emph{privileged} at a configuration $c = (c_0, \ldots, c_{n-1})$
when $f_i(c_{i-1}, c_i, c_{i+1}) \ne c_i$.  A valid \emph{self-stabilizing}
system satisfies six properties: liveness, mutual exclusion, closure,
convergence, fairness, and legitimate-state connectedness (defined
precisely in \cref{sec:prelim}).

Dijkstra exhibited three solutions: a $K$-state uniform construction
($K \ge n$, product $K^n$; Dijkstra's original statement uses $N + 1$
machines and requires $K > N$, which becomes $K \ge n$ for an
$n$-processor ring), a 4-state non-uniform construction (with
the post-1985 caveat that as printed it solves the line, not the ring;
see~\cite{HaddadKnuth1985}), and a 3-state uniform construction with
product $3^n$.  In the winter 1985 Stanford problem
seminar~\cite{HaddadKnuth1985}, Knuth posed the optimization question
exhibited here as our main problem: minimize the state-count product
$m_0 m_1 \cdots m_{n-1}$ over all valid systems on $n$ processors.
Write $\Mn$ for that minimum.  The 1985 seminar pinned $\Mn$ into the
gap $2^n < \Mn \le 3^n$ for $n > 4$, with $\Mn^{1/n}$ growth rate
unsettled.  Knuth recorded two specific asymptotic questions: is
$\limsup_n \Mn^{1/n} < 3$, and is $\liminf_n \Mn^{1/n} > 2$?
Forty years later both are still open.

\subsection{Contributions of this paper}\label{sec:intro-contrib}

\paragraph{Results.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Exact values of $\Mn$ for $n \in \{3, \ldots, 9\}$ in the
central-daemon model ($n \in \{3, 4\}$ unconditional; $n \in \{5,
\ldots, 9\}$ conditional on the replayable lower-bound certificates
of~\cref{app:exhaust}), with the upper bound Lean-checked through
$n = 10$ and the lower bound established by exhaustive computation
through $n = 9$.
\item Non-extension of each witness family past its crossover
point: the small-$n$ absorber family $(2^3, 4, 3^{n-4})$ fails at
every $n = 9$ multiset, and the ternary-strip family sits at
$\Mn$ only at $n = 9$ (\cref{thm:non-extension}).  The arithmetic
$\Mn / M_{n-1}$ break from $3$ to $27/8$ is then a corollary of
the exact values.
\item Ruling out ARG's 1985 LCM bound\footnote{``ARG'' is the
unsigned handle under which the bound appears in the 1985 Stanford
seminar notes~\cite[\S2--\S3]{HaddadKnuth1985}; we use the
seminar's label throughout.} as the mechanism of the
$n = 9$ crossover: ARG's hypothesis excludes every adjacent-binary
witness that realizes $\Mn$ for $n \in \{4, \ldots, 8\}$, and ARG's
LCM functional is constant or strictly nested between $n = 8$ and
$n = 9$ on the non-adjacent orientations it does cover
(\cref{sec:lower-arg}).
\item The conjecture $\Mn = 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ for $n \ge 9$.  It is
established at $n = 9$ (\cref{thm:exact-values}); at $n \in \{10,
11\}$ the upper bound is Lean-checked (through $n = 10$) or
Python-verified CLB (through $n = 18$) and the lower bound is
supported by Axis~C per-probe but \emph{not} proved per-multiset:
all $320$ tested $n = 10$ sub-threshold probes fire Axis~C on
every $(291$-multiset$)$ sub-threshold class, and all $585$ tested
$n = 11$ probes fire across $563$ of $564$ sub-threshold
multisets (one SMT-budget UNKNOWN); per-probe firing under the
DFS $+$ SMT pipeline does not by itself rule out every candidate
good cycle on every multiset, see the scope caveat of
\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}.  At $n \ge 12$ the conjecture is a
conjecture: the formula $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ is realized as an
\emph{upper} bound through $n = 18$ (Lean through $n = 10$,
Python-verified CLB through $n = 18$), making it a plausible
candidate target for the lower bound, but the only direct
lower-bound evidence past $n = 11$ is extrapolation from
corpus-level detector separation on $n \in \{5, \ldots, 10\}$ and
the two full-layer sweeps at $n = 10, 11$.  If
the conjecture is true it forces $\Mn^{1/n} \to 3$ and would
resolve a connected-model variant of Knuth's 1985 asymptotic
questions (\cref{cor:asymptotic}); the unqualified 1985 questions
were posed under the relaxed-connectedness convention and their
resolution requires additional transport results
(\cref{sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}).
\item A z3 SMT certificate that $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_5 = 96$
(\cref{thm:m5rel}): none of the five $n = 5$ sub-threshold
multisets admits a relaxed-valid rule table.  This is the first
case at $n \ge 5$ in which $M_n^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_n$; at $n = 6$,
seven of twelve sub-threshold multisets are UNSAT-certified with
the remaining five returning z3 UNKNOWN within a $3600\,\mathrm{s}$
budget per multiset
(\cref{sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}).
\item A corpus-level ``currency null'' result
(\cref{obs:currency-null}): on the $97$-record stratified corpus,
no dynamical currency (mover-pattern period $T_\mu$,
$\detOf$-coverage $\kappa$, entropy $H$) separates valid from
sub-threshold more cleanly than $\prod m_i$ does, and at $n = 9$
every candidate dynamical currency \emph{flips direction}.  This
rules out the natural reformulation ``lower-bound a different
quantity.''
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Methods.}
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,resume]
\item The CLB ternary-strip witness family on the state vector
$(2, 3, \ldots, 3, 2)$, Python-verified for $n \in \{5, \ldots, 18\}$.
\item A second ternary-strip witness CUP-2 on the same state
vector, Lean 4 / Mathlib formalized for $n \in \{4, \ldots, 10\}$.
\item Structural detectors on candidate good cycles: the
circulation-LP detector separates $93$ of $97$ records in a
stratified corpus (the four failures at the $n = 9$ non-adjacent
$\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$ orientations); the sink-kernel upgrade (Axis C,
\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}) separates all $97$ on the tested

supplies a one-sided sub-threshold certificate on each tested
record.  The corpus spans $n \in \{5, \ldots, 10\}$ on both
sides; the $n = 10$ sub-threshold side is further confirmed by an
extended two-tool (DFS $+$ SMT) sweep that fires Axis C on every
probe the pipeline surfaced, $320 / 320$ probes across every one
of the $291$ $n = 10$ sub-threshold multisets (this is a per-probe
statement, not a per-multiset completeness claim; see the scope
caveat in \cref{sec:detector-axis-c}).
\end{enumerate}

A systematic diagnostic catalog of failed analytical lower-bound
approaches across five invariant families (\cref{sec:landscape})
accompanies these contributions as a service to the community.

\subsection{Why this paper}\label{sec:intro-why}

In 1985, Knuth~\cite{HaddadKnuth1985} asked how small the product of
state-counts can be on a self-stabilizing ring.  Forty years later,
the question is still open in both directions: Dijkstra's $3^n$ is
the best known upper bound, and the 1985 seminar's $2^n$ is the
best known lower bound.  This paper closes the gap exactly for
$n \le 9$ and proposes $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ as the answer for
$n \ge 9$.

\paragraph{Why now.}  Four tooling shifts make the $n \le 9$
frontier tractable in 2026 in a way it was not in 1985 or 2005.
First, modern SMT solvers dispatch rule-table feasibility on
sub-threshold multisets in seconds to hours where exhaustive
rule-table enumeration requires $10^{10}$--$10^{12}$ candidates
per multiset (\cref{sec:main-relaxed,sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}).
Second, Lean 4 / Mathlib now supports finite per-$n$ rank-table
convergence certificates sealed by \texttt{native\_decide} at
state-counts up to $26244$ (\cref{app:lean}).  Third, consumer
compute makes exhaustive lower-bound search at $n = 9$
(tens of thousands of multiset/orientation classes) a
hours-to-days job rather than a decades job.  Fourth, and what
changes the feasible \emph{shape} of the inquiry rather than only
its speed, frontier LLMs (Claude Opus, GPT-5.4) run as
specialist research agents under a residue-prompt protocol
(\texttt{docs/residue\_prompt\_v2.md}) and an RA/PA/LE division
of labour (\texttt{docs/ra\_le\_workflow.md}), letting a single
human orchestrator run, log, and cross-check the probe volume
required to chart the failed-approach landscape catalogued in
\cref{sec:landscape,app:landscape-detail}: $\sim\!2{,}100$ Python
research probes ($\sim\!633{,}000$ lines) carrying $721$ logged
explorations across $30$ chronological logs, plus the
$\sim\!29{,}000$-line Lean formalization and the $\sim\!79{,}000$
lines of Lean \texttt{attic/} preserving failed routes.  The probe
volume is load-bearing: the detector hierarchy of
\cref{sec:detector-axis-c} and the obstruction catalog of
\cref{sec:landscape} both require exhausting a \emph{space} of
candidates, not verifying a single one, and that space could not
have been swept at this density by unassisted human enumeration in
the available calendar time.  None of these four shifts was
available to the 1985 seminar; none has previously been applied
to this problem.

To our knowledge, no prior work has determined $\Mn$ exactly beyond
$M_4$ in the central-daemon model; identified the sharp ratio
crossover at $n = 9$ (in the sense of \cref{rem:phase-scope});
produced machine-verified constructions at this scale; documented
a systematic analytical-obstruction catalog; or produced structural
detectors separating the $n \le 10$ sub-threshold corpus from the
valid-witness corpus with a one-sided certificate via sink-kernel
monotonicity on the forced-NG digraph
(\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}).

\section{Preliminaries}\label{sec:prelim}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{The ring model}\label{sec:prelim-ring}

A \emph{ring} consists of $n$ processors $P_0, \ldots, P_{n-1}$ with
state counts $\mathbf{m} = (m_0, \ldots, m_{n-1})$, each $m_i \ge 2$,
and local transition functions
\[
   f_i : \mathrm{Fin}(m_{i-1}) \times \mathrm{Fin}(m_i) \times \mathrm{Fin}(m_{i+1}) \to \mathrm{Fin}(m_i),
   \qquad i \in \{0, \ldots, n-1\},
\]
with indices modulo $n$. A \emph{configuration} is a tuple
$c = (c_0, \ldots, c_{n-1}) \in \Config(\mathbf{m}) := \prod_i \mathrm{Fin}(m_i)$.
Processor $P_i$ is \emph{privileged} at $c$ when
$f_i(c_{i-1}, c_i, c_{i+1}) \ne c_i$.  A \emph{move} from $c$ at a
privileged $P_i$ replaces $c_i$ by $f_i(c_{i-1}, c_i, c_{i+1})$ and
leaves the other coordinates unchanged.  Under the \emph{central daemon}
the next-step move is one privileged $P_i$ chosen non-deterministically.

\subsection{Model convention}\label{sec:prelim-model}

\textbf{Convention.} Throughout this paper, \emph{validity} means
Dijkstra's central-daemon model with the six properties of
\cref{sec:prelim-five} (liveness, mutual exclusion, closure, convergence,
fairness, legitimate-state connectedness).  The connectedness property
is the one carried over from Dijkstra's original 1974
paper~\cite[\S3]{Dijkstra1974}, which required that for any two
legitimate states there exist a sequence of moves transferring one to
the other.  Under this requirement the legitimate states form a single
orbit under the unique good move, so the Gray-code multi-cycle
constructions studied in the 1985 seminar do not qualify.

This is a model choice, not a correction.  Historical references to
$M_4 = 16$~\cite[p.~70]{HaddadKnuth1985} should be read under the
\emph{relaxed-connectedness convention} of the 1985 seminar (credit
due to Knuth), in which Dijkstra's legitimate-state connectedness
requirement was dropped and the good configurations were allowed to
split into several disjoint good cycles; in the central-daemon model
of this paper (which retains Dijkstra's connectedness), $M_4 = 24$
(\cref{thm:exact-values}).  Later references to the same convention
use \emph{relaxed-connectedness} or \emph{1985 convention} without
further qualifier.

\subsection{The six validity properties}\label{sec:prelim-five}

A system $(\mathbf{m}, f_0, \ldots, f_{n-1})$ is \emph{valid} when:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{Liveness.} In every configuration at least one processor is privileged.
\item \textbf{Mutual exclusion.} In every \emph{good} configuration
(see~below) exactly one processor is privileged.
\item \textbf{Closure.} The unique move from a good configuration leads
to another good configuration.
\item \textbf{Convergence.} No daemon schedule starting from any bad
configuration cycles back to itself; equivalently, every execution
reaches a good configuration in finitely many steps.
\item \textbf{Fairness.} Every cycle of moves through good configurations
fires every processor at least once.
\item \textbf{Legitimate-state connectedness.} For any two good
configurations $g, h$ there is a sequence of good moves leading from
$g$ to $h$.  Equivalently (given the preceding properties), the good
configurations form a single directed cycle under the unique good
move.
\end{enumerate}
Configurations are partitioned by the designer into \emph{good} (mutual
exclusion holds, the designer's certificate of correct service) and
\emph{bad} (transient).  Property~(6) is Dijkstra's original
legitimate-state connectedness
requirement~\cite[\S3]{Dijkstra1974}.  The 1985 Stanford
seminar~\cite[p.~70]{HaddadKnuth1985} worked with a relaxed version
of property (6) in which the legitimate states were allowed to
split into several disjoint good cycles; under that convention
the $M_4 = 16$ Gray-code construction (which splits the $16$
legitimate states into two disjoint $8$-cycles) is admissible.
This paper retains Dijkstra's original connectedness requirement,
under which $M_4 = 24$ (\cref{thm:M4-eq-24}).

\subsection{Dijkstra's three solutions}\label{sec:prelim-dijkstra}

Dijkstra's $K$-state uniform construction~\cite{Dijkstra1974} achieves
product $K^n$ for $K \ge n$ (Dijkstra's original statement uses $N+1$
machines and requires $K > N$; translating to a ring of $n = N + 1$
processors gives $K \ge n$).  His 4-state non-uniform construction
achieves product $4^{n-1}$ on a line; as the 1985 seminar
documented~\cite[p.~76]{HaddadKnuth1985}, this construction does not
close into a ring without modification.  Dijkstra's 3-state uniform
construction (Solution 3) achieves product $3^n$, with the convergence
proof appearing only in 1986~\cite{Dijkstra1986}.  Solution 3 is the
only one of the three whose product matches the trivial uniform
$\Mn^{1/n} \to 3$ asymptotic without further analysis.

\subsection{The state-product minimum $\Mn$}\label{sec:prelim-Mn}

We write
\[
   \Mn \,:=\, \min \Bigl\{ \textstyle\prod_{i=0}^{n-1} m_i \,:\, (\mathbf{m}, f_0, \ldots, f_{n-1}) \text{ is valid in the sense of \cref{sec:prelim-five}} \Bigr\}
\]
for $n \ge 3$, where validity includes the legitimate-state
connectedness property~(6).  The connected-model analogues of
Knuth's two open questions~\cite[p.~68]{HaddadKnuth1985} ask whether
$\limsup_n \Mn^{1/n} < 3$ and whether $\liminf_n \Mn^{1/n} > 2$; the
original 1985 questions were posed under the relaxed-connectedness
convention of \cref{sec:prelim-model}, and transporting any bound
from the connected model to that convention is an open problem
(\cref{sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}).

\subsection{Two distinct length quantities}\label{sec:prelim-lengths}

Two quantities associated with the good cycle of a valid system both
deserve to be called ``length.''  We will keep them rigidly distinct:

\begin{definition}\label{def:lengths}
The \emph{good-configuration count} $L$ is the number of distinct
configurations on the good cycle.  The \emph{mover-pattern period}
$T_\mu$ is the period of the cyclic sequence of firing processor
indices around the good cycle.
\end{definition}

These are not in general equal.  Both ternary-strip constructions of
\cref{sec:upper} have $T_\mu = 3n - 2$, but the CLB construction has
$L = n^2 - 2n + 8$ while the CUP-2 construction has
$L = (n+2)(n+3)/2 - 5$.

\subsection{Sub-threshold terminology}\label{sec:prelim-subthreshold}

We will repeatedly compare a candidate state-count vector against the
proved or conjectured target product at each $n$.

\begin{definition}\label{def:Bn}
For $n \ge 5$ define
\[
   \Bn \,:=\, \begin{cases}
       32 \cdot 3^{\,n-4} & \text{if } 5 \le n \le 8 \quad (\text{the proved exact value } \Mn),\\[2pt]
       4 \cdot 3^{\,n-2}  & \text{if } n \ge 9      \quad (\text{the proved exact value } \Mn \text{ at } n = 9; \text{ the large-}n\text{ conjectured target}).
   \end{cases}
\]
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:record}
A \emph{detector record} is a pair $(\mathbf{m}, gc)$ where
$\mathbf{m}$ is a state-count vector and $gc$ is a candidate good cycle
on $\mathbf{m}$ (a closed sequence of distinct configurations with
exactly one privileged processor at each step and fairness).  For
\emph{valid-system records}, $gc$ is the good cycle of a verified
valid system.  For \emph{candidate records} no valid extension is
assumed.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}\label{def:thresholds}
A detector record $(\mathbf{m}, gc)$ is
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{sub-threshold} if $\prod_i m_i < \Bn$;
\item a \emph{$\Bn$-threshold witness} if it is a valid-system record with $\prod_i m_i = \Bn$ (proved sharp only when $\Bn = \Mn$ has been established);
\item a \emph{valid super-threshold witness} if it is a valid-system record with $\prod_i m_i > \Bn$.
\end{itemize}
\end{definition}

We avoid the unqualified phrase ``sharp valid witness'' for any record
where the lower bound has not been proved.

\subsection{Structural objects used later}\label{sec:prelim-objects}

The lower-bound discussion of \cref{sec:detector,sec:landscape} requires
several derived objects, defined here in summary form (full Lean-level
definitions are in the source repository under
\texttt{LeanMn/LowerBound/}, with a glossary in the supplementary
lower-bound history memo listed in \cref{app:manifest}).

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Given a candidate good cycle $gc = [c_0, \ldots, c_{L-1}]$, the
\emph{determined dictionary} $\detOf(gc)$ is the partial rule table read
off the cycle: for each context $(i, l, s, r)$ realized at some step
$k$, $\detOf(gc)$ records $c_{k+1}(i)$.  Stay-entries (where $c_{k+1}(i) = c_k(i)$)
and move-entries (where they differ) are tracked separately.
\item $\NGset(gc)$ denotes the set of \emph{non-good configurations}
(those not appearing on $gc$).
\item The \emph{Hamming-1 tube} $T_{N_1}(gc)$ is the set of
configurations at Hamming distance exactly $1$ from some
$c_k$ that are also \emph{value-consistent} with $gc$
(every coordinate value appears at the corresponding position somewhere on $gc$).
\item The \emph{forced graph} on a set $S \subseteq \NGset(gc)$ has edges
$c \to c'$ whenever $c$ has a forced successor $c' \in S$ under
$\detOf(gc)$.
\item The \emph{sink kernel} $\SK(gc)$ is the fixed point of iteratively
removing sinks (vertices with no out-edge) from the forced graph on
$\NGset(gc)$.
\item The \emph{lifted-defect graph} on $gc$ has vertex set
$V_{\mathrm{lift}}(gc) = \{(k, q, a) : a \ne c_k(q),\ c_k[q := a] \in T_{N_1}(gc)\}$
with the edge taxonomy of \cref{sec:detector}.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Exact values}\label{sec:main-exact}

\begin{theorem}[Exact values, central-daemon model]\label{thm:exact-values}
In Dijkstra's central-daemon model of \cref{sec:prelim-five}, the
minimum state product takes the values
\[
\begin{array}{c|c|l}
n & \Mn & \text{factorization}\\\hline
3 & 8     & 2^3\\
4 & 24    & 2^3 \cdot 3\\
5 & 96    & 2^5 \cdot 3\\
6 & 288   & 2^5 \cdot 3^2\\
7 & 864   & 2^5 \cdot 3^3\\
8 & 2592  & 2^5 \cdot 3^4\\
9 & 8748  & 2^2 \cdot 3^7
\end{array}
\]
Each row combines an upper-bound witness with a lower-bound
certificate (\cref{sec:upper,sec:lower}).  The upper bounds for
$n \in \{4, \ldots, 10\}$ are Lean-checked
(\cref{thm:upper-bound-cert,thm:upper-bound-small,thm:M4-eq-24}).
The lower bounds are certificate-backed as follows: $n = 3$ by
trivial enumeration (no sub-threshold multisets exist); $n = 4$
analytically by \cref{thm:M4-eq-24}; $n \in \{5, \ldots, 8\}$ by
the exhaustive-search procedure of \cref{app:exhaust}, whose
driver is shipped and whose per-orientation rejection stream is
pre-generated for $n \in \{3, 4\}$ and the $n = 5$ all-binary
orientation, and regenerable from a clean clone for any other
orientation (\cref{app:exhaust-artifact,app:exhaust-repro}); and
$n = 9$ by the targeted absorber-specific computational
probes of \cref{sec:lower-exhaustive} on the $3$ multisets at
product $7776$ and the $24$ multisets at products in
$(7776, 8748)$.  The rows $n \in \{4, \ldots, 9\}$ are sharp;
sharpness at $n = 10$ is conjectural (\cref{sec:main-conj}).
\end{theorem}

\paragraph{Certificate-backing status.} The $n = 3$ row is
unconditional (trivial enumeration); $n = 4$ is unconditional via
\cref{thm:M4-eq-24}.  For $n \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$, the $\ge$
direction of \cref{thm:exact-values} is backed by the exhaustive
search of \cref{app:exhaust}.  The deterministic coverage manifest
(C1 + C2, overall hash \texttt{f684ed53216a0d7f}) ships for every
$n \in \{3, \ldots, 9\}$ under \texttt{artifacts/rejections/} in
the release tag (\cref{app:exhaust-artifact}); the
per-certificate rejection stream ships in full for
$n \in \{3, 4\}$ and for the all-binary orientation at $n = 5$
as a worked example of the search mechanism.  Per-cert streams
for the remaining $n = 5$ orientations and for all
$n \in \{6, \ldots, 9\}$ are regenerable from a clean clone with
the driver of \cref{app:exhaust-repro} (the full stream at
worst-case multisets is prohibitive in disk size, which is why
it is not pre-generated); see the reproduction map in
\texttt{REVIEWER.md} (\cref{sec:intro-repo}).

\subsection{The ratio crossover at $n = 9$}\label{sec:main-phase}

Given \cref{thm:exact-values}, the ratio
identity is arithmetic.

\begin{observation}[Ratio crossover]\label{obs:ratio-crossover}
Conditional on the same replayable certificates as
\cref{thm:exact-values}, the consecutive ratios are
\[
   \frac{M_5}{M_4} = 4, \qquad
   \frac{M_n}{M_{n-1}} = 3 \text{ for } n \in \{6, 7, 8\}, \qquad
   \frac{M_9}{M_8} = \frac{27}{8}.
\]
Both endpoints of the constant-$3$ plateau are structural: $M_5/M_4
= 4$ is the absorber family turning on (quaternary enters at
$n = 5$), and $M_9/M_8 = 27/8$ is the absorber family turning off.
\end{observation}

The non-trivial statement is that neither witness family realizes
$\Mn$ past its own crossover point: the small-$n$ absorber
multiset $(2^3, 4, 3^{n-4})$ (\cref{sec:upper-small}) fails at every
$n = 9$ multiset, and the ternary-strip formula $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$
is sharp only at $n = 9$ among currently proved exact values.

\begin{theorem}[Non-extension of each family]\label{thm:non-extension}
Conditional on the replayable lower-bound certificates of
\cref{app:exhaust}:
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item For every multiset $\mathbf{m}$ at $n = 9$ with $\prod m_i =
32 \cdot 3^{\,9-4} = 7776$, namely the three small-$n$
absorber shapes $\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$, $\{2^4, 3^4, 6\}$, and
$\{2^5, 3^3, 9\}$ (\cref{tab:n9-7776}), no valid rule table
exists (\cref{sec:lower-exhaustive}).
\item The ternary-strip product $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ is sharp at
$n = 9$ (\cref{thm:exact-values}) and realized by verified
witnesses at $n \in \{4, \ldots, 10\}$ via CUP-2
(\cref{thm:upper-bound-cert}); at $n \le 8$
it is strictly above $\Mn$ by a factor of $\frac{4\cdot 3^{n-2}}{32\cdot 3^{n-4}} = 9/8$.
\end{enumerate}
Sharpness of the ternary-strip product at $n = 10$ is conjectural.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}[Terminology]\label{rem:phase-scope}
We informally refer to the crossover of
\cref{obs:ratio-crossover,thm:non-extension} as a \emph{phase
transition}.  The term is used only in this informal sense; we do
\emph{not} claim to have identified a structural mechanism that
\emph{causes} the break at $n = 9$ specifically.  A uniform
mechanistic explanation is open (\cref{sec:lower-arg}), and in
particular ARG's 1985 LCM bound does not account for it.
\end{remark}

\subsection{The large-$n$ conjecture}\label{sec:main-conj}

\begin{conjecture}[Upper bound, all $n \ge 9$]\label{conj:ub-allN}
For every $n \ge 9$ there exists a valid rule table on the
ternary-strip state vector $(2, 3, 3, \ldots, 3, 2)$, giving the upper
bound $\Mn \,\le\, 4 \cdot 3^{\,n-2}$.  The CLB and CUP-2 rule tables of
\cref{sec:upper} are two candidate families realizing this state vector
in the verified finite ranges.
\end{conjecture}

Status: Lean-checked for $n \in \{4, \ldots, 10\}$ via CUP-2
(\cref{thm:upper-bound-cert}); Python-verified for
$n \in \{5, \ldots, 18\}$ via the CLB construction.  For $n \ge 19$
only Dijkstra's classical
$\Mn \le 3^n$~\cite{Dijkstra1974} is currently available, a
factor $9/4 \approx 2.25$ looser than the conjectured
$4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$.

\begin{conjecture}[Lower bound, all $n \ge 9$]\label{conj:lb-allN}
Every valid system on $n \ge 9$ processors has
$\prod_i m_i \,\ge\, 4 \cdot 3^{\,n-2}$.
\end{conjecture}

Status of \cref{conj:lb-allN}, stratified by regime:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item $n = 9$ is \textbf{established} by exhaustive computation
(\cref{thm:exact-values,sec:lower-exhaustive}): every sub-target
product class is eliminated by a replayable certificate shipped
with the release tag (\cref{app:exhaust-artifact}).
\item $n \in \{10, 11\}$ is \textbf{Axis~C-supported, not proved}:
no exhaustive certificate search has been run, but the DFS $+$ SMT
sweep fires Axis C on all $320$ tested probes across the $291$
sub-threshold multisets at $n = 10$ and on all $585$ probes
across $563$ of $564$ sub-threshold multisets at $n = 11$ (one
SMT-budget UNKNOWN), which eliminates each of them under
sink-kernel monotonicity \emph{on the tested records}
(\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}).
\item $n \ge 12$ is \textbf{conjecture}.  Supporting evidence is
(a) two Lean- and Python-verified witness families realizing
$4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ (through $n = 10$ and $n = 18$ respectively), and
(b) corpus-level detector separation on $10$ valid witnesses and
$87$ sub-threshold candidates spanning $n \in \{5, \ldots, 10\}$,
extended by two full-layer sub-threshold sweeps at $n = 10, 11$.
No analytical lower-bound argument is available.
\end{itemize}

The formula $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ is \emph{false} as a lower bound at
$n \in \{5, \ldots, 8\}$, where the small-$n$ absorber witnesses
give $32 \cdot 3^{n-4} < 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$, which is why the
conjecture starts at $n = 9$.

\begin{corollary}[Joint consequence, conditional]\label{cor:asymptotic}
If \Cref{conj:ub-allN,conj:lb-allN} both hold, then $\Mn = 4 \cdot
3^{n-2}$ for $n \ge 9$, hence $\Mn^{1/n} \to 3$.  This would
resolve a natural connected-model variant of Knuth's 1985
asymptotic questions: the $\limsup < 3$ variant negatively and the
$\liminf > 2$ variant positively.  It does \emph{not}, on its own,
resolve Knuth's unqualified 1985 questions, which were posed under
the relaxed-connectedness convention; transporting any of the
bounds of this paper to that convention is an open problem
(\cref{sec:prelim-model,sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}), and at
the one case beyond $n = 4$ where both values are now known
($n = 5$, \cref{thm:m5rel}) the two conventions agree.
\end{corollary}

Evidence for \cref{conj:lb-allN} is the subject of
\cref{sec:detector,sec:landscape}.

\subsection{Transport to the relaxed-connectedness convention, first data}\label{sec:main-relaxed}

The 1985 Stanford seminar~\cite{HaddadKnuth1985} worked under a
relaxation of Dijkstra's property (6) in which the legitimate
states were allowed to split into several disjoint good cycles
(\cref{sec:prelim-model}).  Write $\Mn^{\mathrm{rel}}$ for the
minimum state product under that convention.  Every
connected-valid system is also relaxed-valid, so
$\Mn^{\mathrm{rel}} \le \Mn$; the reverse direction is open at
every $n \ge 5$ and has stood open since the 1985 formulation.

\begin{theorem}[$M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = 96$, SMT-certified]\label{thm:m5rel}
$M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_5 = 96$.
\end{theorem}

The proof is a z3 SMT encoding of the five validity properties in
the relaxed convention: at each of the five $n = 5$ sub-threshold
multisets $\{2^5\}, \{2^4, 3\}, \{2^4, 4\}, \{2^3, 3^2\}, \{2^4, 5\}$
the encoding returns UNSAT in under $90\,\mathrm{s}$ on consumer
hardware, and an at-threshold SAT check on $\{2^3, 3, 4\}$
($\prod m = 96 = M_5$) confirms the encoding is correct.

\paragraph{Encoding of property (4).}  Property (4)
(convergence) is infinitary: ``no daemon schedule starting from
a bad configuration cycles back to itself.''  The SMT encoding
reformulates it in the standard rank-function style: the solver is
asked to exhibit a per-configuration rank function $\rho :
\Config(\mathbf{m}) \to \mathrm{Fin}(B)$ with a bound $B$
polynomial in $\prod m_i$, such that every bad-move $c \to c'$
satisfies $\rho(c') < \rho(c)$.  Existence of such a $\rho$ is a
first-order condition on the $\mathrm{Fin}(m_i)$-valued rule
tables and is equivalent to finite termination from every bad
configuration.  UNSAT at a sub-threshold multiset then means
``no relaxed-valid rule table admits such a rank function,''
which is the same as ``no relaxed-valid rule table exists'' given
finite state.  The soundness assumption is standard: termination
on a finite transition system is witnessed by a decreasing rank,
and the encoding chooses $B$ large enough that the rank is
expressive.  The at-threshold SAT check extracts a rank and
re-runs the extracted rule table through the Python relaxed
verifier, which closes the encoding/semantics gap end-to-end.

The
per-multiset UNSAT table, runtimes, and the encoding source are
shipped with the repository bundle; the detailed discussion is
deferred to \cref{sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}.

At $n = 6$ seven of the twelve sub-threshold multisets are
UNSAT-certified under the same encoding, including the hardest-so-far
$\{2^3, 3^3\}$ (UNSAT in $\approx 1900\,\mathrm{s}$) and
$\{2^4, 3, 4\}$ (UNSAT in $\approx 2170\,\mathrm{s}$); the remaining
five (at products $192, 224, 240, 256, 256$) return z3 UNKNOWN
within a $3600\,\mathrm{s}$ per-multiset budget and require either a
stronger WLOG symmetry break, a portfolio of solvers, or a tighter
fairness encoding for certification.  \cref{thm:m5rel} is the first case at
$n \ge 5$ in which $M_n^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_n$ has been shown (at
$n = 4$ the conventions disagree, $M_4^{\mathrm{rel}} \le 16 <
24 = M_4$).  Equivalently, \cref{thm:m5rel} resolves the question
of whether $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} < M_5$, in the negative.

\section{Upper-bound constructions}\label{sec:upper}
% =====================================================================

This section describes the witness families supporting the conjectured
bound $\Mn \le 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ for $n \ge 9$, with Lean certification
through $n = 10$ and Python verification through $n = 18$.  For
$n \ge 19$ the paper falls back on Dijkstra's classical
$\Mn \le 3^n$~\cite{Dijkstra1974}.  For $n \in \{5, \ldots, 8\}$ a
distinct, tighter family supplies $\Mn \le 32 \cdot 3^{n-4}$.  We
begin with the unconditional base case $n = 4$ in miniature.

\subsection{A fully worked example: $n = 4$}\label{sec:upper-n4}

Before describing the infinite witness families we assemble the
entire $n = 4$ certificate in one place.  The case is unconditional
(\cref{thm:M4-eq-24}) and illustrates in miniature every object
used later.

\paragraph{State vector.} $\mathbf{m} = (2, 2, 2, 3)$: three binary
processors at positions $0, 1, 2$ and one ternary processor at
position $3$.  Product $24$.

\paragraph{Upper bound ($M_4 \le 24$).} A verified good cycle on
$(2, 2, 2, 3)$ together with a rank-based convergence certificate
(a per-configuration potential $\rho : \Config(\mathbf{m}) \to
\mathbb{N}$ strictly decreasing on every bad-configuration move)
gives $M_4 \le 24$.  The full transition table, the cycle in
configuration form, and the rank potential are emitted by the
parity runner of \cref{app:lean-encoding-audit} and checked by
\texttt{M\_4\_upper} in \texttt{LeanMn/SmallN/Theorem.lean}.

\paragraph{Lower bound ($M_4 \ge 24$).} The only multiset with
$\prod m_i < 24$ and each $m_i \ge 2$ on four positions is
$(2, 2, 2, 2)$ at product $16$.  A direct case analysis on
$(2, 2, 2, 2)$ in the six-property connected model eliminates every
candidate good cycle: any good cycle on $\{0, 1\}^4$ would have to
visit $16$ distinct configurations, but the connectedness
requirement (property (6)) together with mutual exclusion forces
the good cycle to be a single Hamiltonian cycle on $\{0,1\}^4$, and
no rule table satisfying both closure and convergence is
extensible to such a cycle.  Under the relaxed-connectedness
convention of the 1985 seminar the same multiset admits the
historical Gray-code witness $M_4^{\mathrm{rel}} \le 16$
\cite[p.~70]{HaddadKnuth1985}; the two conventions disagree at
$n = 4$ precisely because property (6) excludes the Gray-code from
the connected model.  The Lean proof is \texttt{M\_4\_lower}; the
bundle is \texttt{M\_4\_eq\_24}.

\paragraph{What this case illustrates.} The $n = 4$ row of
\cref{thm:exact-values} is the only one beyond $n = 3$ that is
unconditional (Lean-proved end-to-end without an
exhaustive-search certificate); the rows
$n \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$ (\cref{thm:exact-values}) depend
on \cref{app:exhaust}'s exhaustive-search certificates.  It is also
the smallest instance of the
relaxed-vs-connected gap studied in
\cref{sec:main-relaxed,sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}.  The
ingredients (explicit good cycle, rank potential, case analysis
eliminating sub-threshold multisets) reappear in every later
construction; at $n \ge 5$ the last two scale up into the
convergence rank tables of \cref{sec:upper-cup2} and the
exhaustive-search certificates of \cref{app:exhaust} respectively.

quaternary processor}, and ternary fill at the remaining positions.
In the stored witnesses the binary processors are in some cases
clustered into a $3$-consecutive block and in other cases include

\subsection{The CUP-2 Lean-checked variant}\label{sec:upper-cup2}

The same ternary-strip state vector $(2, 3, \ldots, 3, 2)$ supports a
second valid rule table, \emph{CUP-2} (short for \emph{Convergent
Universal Primer, second iteration}; the name is inherited from the
construction's internal label in the Lean formalization and carries
no mathematical content beyond identifying the rule table), distinct
from the CLB rule table of \cref{sec:upper-clb}.  CUP-2 has the same
mover-pattern period $T_\mu = 3n - 2$; its good-configuration count is
$L = (n+2)(n+3)/2 - 5$.  The rule table has $87$ entries that are
$n$-independent.  Each entry is tagged by up to three non-exclusive
per-entry properties, namely:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{privileged}: the entry is a move-entry (it fires on
the good cycle, writing a new value different from the current
center coordinate);
\item \emph{copy-neighbor}: the output equals the left or right
neighbour's current value (a local-propagation entry);
\item \emph{anomalous}: the entry falls outside the two cleanest
ternary-strip idiom families and required manual fit to close
convergence.
\end{itemize}
A single entry may satisfy more than one property (for instance, a
privileged entry that writes the value of a neighbour is both
privileged and copy-neighbor); the three properties partition neither
the rule table nor the privileged entries.  The per-property counts
across all $87$ entries are $45$ privileged, $40$ copy-neighbor, and
$5$ anomalous; these sums exceed $87$ precisely because the
categories overlap.

CUP-2 is a distinct ternary-strip rule table with a structurally
cleaner convergence certificate; its good-cycle count is comparable
to, and for $n \ge 6$ strictly smaller than, the CLB good-cycle count
($L_{\mathrm{CUP}} - L_{\mathrm{CLB}} = -(n-4)(n-5)/2$, zero at
$n \in \{4, 5\}$ and negative thereafter).  The Lean formalization
uses a per-$n$ rank function on bad configurations that strictly
decreases on every bad step, sealed by \texttt{native\_decide} on a
precomputed rank table.

\begin{theorem}[CUP-2 upper bound, Lean-checked]\label{thm:upper-bound-cert}
\texttt{upper\_bound\_cert} in \texttt{LeanMn/UpperBound/Theorem.lean}
proves $\Mn \le 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ for $n \in \{4, \ldots, 10\}$
(sorry-free; finite per-$n$ rank-table checks discharged by
\texttt{native\_decide} on sealed certificates).
\end{theorem}

\section{Exact lower bounds at small $n$}\label{sec:lower}
% =====================================================================

This section certifies the $\ge$ direction of
\cref{thm:exact-values}.  The methodology
and the per-$n$ enumeration are described here in summary; the full
reproducibility material lives in \cref{app:exhaust}.

\subsection{Methodology (certificate-grade)}\label{sec:lower-method}

For each $n \in \{3, \ldots, 9\}$ we proceed as follows.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Enumerate all state-count vectors with product $T$ satisfying
$T < \Mn$ (strictly below the claimed minimum; candidates at $T = \Mn$
are excluded so the actual sharp witness is not rejected).
\item Quotient by cyclic rotation, reflection, and state-renaming
symmetries where sound.
\item Enumerate candidate good cycles and partial rule tables within
each class.
\item Apply sound pruning rules (quasi-unidirectionality,
forced-neighbor constraints, $\detOf$-dictionary consistency).
\item For every surviving candidate, verify that no completion to a
full transition function satisfies the six validity properties of
\cref{sec:prelim-five}.
\item Produce machine-checkable failure certificates or independently
replayable logs.
\item Validate the search with an independent checker (a re-implementation
in a second code path, \cref{app:exhaust}).
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}\label{rem:proof-obligation}
The $\ge$ direction of
\cref{thm:exact-values} is \emph{not}
merely the statement that the search failed to find a witness; it
is that every sub-target product class is eliminated by a sound,
replayable certificate.  \Cref{app:exhaust} is the proof obligation
for $n \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$, discharged by the deterministic
exhaustive-search driver; the pre-generated rejection stream at
\texttt{artifacts/rejections/} ships in full for
$n \in \{3, 4\}$ and for the all-binary orientation at $n = 5$,
with per-cert streams at every other $(n, \text{orientation})$ pair
regenerable from a clean clone (\cref{app:exhaust-artifact},
\cref{app:exhaust-repro}).
\end{remark}

\subsection{The exhaustive verification at $n \in \{3, \ldots, 9\}$}\label{sec:lower-exhaustive}

All state-count vectors with $\prod_i m_i < \Mn$ are eliminated by the
above method.  In particular at $n = 9$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item every multiset with $\prod_i m_i = 7776 = 32 \cdot 3^5$ fails:
the three multisets $\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$, $\{2^4, 3^4, 6\}$, and
$\{2^5, 3^3, 9\}$ (\cref{app:exhaust}, \cref{tab:n9-7776}, lists
every such multiset and its dihedral orientation count; total $94$
orientations);
\item every multiset with $\prod_i m_i \in (7776,\, 8748)$ fails:
\cref{app:exhaust}, \cref{tab:n9-strict-between}, lists all $24$ such
multisets with their products;
\item the state vector $(2, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2)$ at product $8748$
is a verified valid system (CLB witness, \cref{sec:upper-clb};
also CUP-2 witness, \cref{sec:upper-cup2}).
\end{itemize}
The three $7776$ multisets and the $24$ multisets in
$(7776, 8748)$ were discharged by targeted computational probes
on these absorber-specific candidates rather than by running the
general App C rejection stream end-to-end (the latter is
feasible in principle at $n = 9$ on local hardware but the
per-multiset per-orientation wall-clock and output-size cost make
pre-generation of the full bundle impractical; \cref{app:exhaust-repro}).
The probes themselves are deterministic Python scripts shipped
under \texttt{docs/axisc/} and \texttt{probes/}; their outputs
(per-orientation verdicts and Farkas-style infeasibility
witnesses where available) are archived as JSON alongside the
source.  Hence $M_9 = 8748$ exactly.  The same App C
methodology, applied at the smaller target products
$\Mn = 32 \cdot 3^{n-4}$ for $n \in \{5, 6, 7, 8\}$ and
$\Mn = 24$ for $n = 4$ and $\Mn = 8$ for $n = 3$, certifies the
remaining rows of \cref{thm:exact-values} (with the $n = 4$ row
also discharged analytically in Lean as
\cref{thm:M4-eq-24}).

State-space size grows as $\prod m$, and the number of candidate good
cycles, partial rule tables, and completions grows substantially faster
than that.  Beyond $n = 9$ the certificate search exceeds available
hardware budget under the current pruning rules; this is why
\cref{conj:lb-allN} is open for $n \ge 10$ (\cref{sec:lower-no-extend}).

\subsection{ARG's bound and the $n = 9$ crossover}\label{sec:lower-arg}

The $n = 9$ ratio crossover is \emph{not} explained by ARG's 1985
LCM bound.  Two observations
suffice~\cite[\S2--\S3]{HaddadKnuth1985}; a self-contained dispatch
is shipped with the repository bundle
(\cref{app:exhaust-artifact}).
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Scope.} The small-$n$ absorber witnesses use adjacent
binary positions (\cref{sec:upper-small}), which lie outside ARG's
non-adjacency hypothesis.  ARG's bound is silent on every known valid
construction in this family.
\item \textbf{Insensitivity.} On the non-adjacent orientations of
$\{2^3, 4, 3^{n-4}\}$ that ARG does cover, the LCM functional is
constant or strictly nested between $n = 8$ and $n = 9$ under each of
four natural readings of ``LCM of block state counts'' (global,
per-arc-min, per-arc-max, block-product).  A shipped script (\texttt{arg\_lcm\_finite\_checks.py};
\cref{app:manifest}) enumerates the dihedral orbit of non-adjacent
orientations ($6$ at
$n = 8$, $10$ at $n = 9$) and reports for each reading the set of
values taken across the orbit; the verified numerics are
\emph{global-LCM} $\{12\} = \{12\}$, \emph{per-arc-min-LCM}
$\{3, 12\} = \{3, 12\}$, \emph{per-arc-max-LCM} $\{12\} = \{12\}$,
and \emph{block-products} $\{3, 4, 9, 12, 27, 36\} \subset \{3, 4, 9,
12, 27, 36, 81, 108\}$ (first set at $n = 8$, second at $n = 9$).  No
LCM-functional reading discriminates the phase-transition boundary.
\end{enumerate}

\begin{remark}[ARG's bound remains a genuine 1985 contribution]\label{rem:arg-not-wrong}
Within its stated non-adjacency hypothesis ARG's result is correct and
useful: it reproves all-binary impossibility for $n > 4$
($L = 1$ forces $N = 0$) and caps the binary count at $4$ in the
all-ternary-non-binary mixed case $\{2^k, 3^{n-k}\}$ ($L = 3$ forces
$k \le 4$).  These are scope-limits on the witness search; they
simply do not locate the $n = 9$ boundary.
\end{remark}

The 1985 seminar's quasi-unidirectionality bound (no $4$ or more
consecutive binary processors~\cite[p.~75]{HaddadKnuth1985}) is
compatible with the crossover but silent on the $3$-consecutive case
at $n \ge 9$.  Candidate analytical directions for future work include
palindromic entry conflict, shadow-cycle mirror arguments, and
good-cycle-length versus product entropy bounds; we treat the mechanism
as an open structural problem.

\subsection{Why exhaustive search does not extend to $n \ge 10$}\label{sec:lower-no-extend}

At $n = 10$, the target state space itself has size $26244$, but
exhaustive lower-bound certification requires eliminating many
state-count vectors, candidate good cycles, partial rule tables, and
completions below the target.  Under the current pruning rules, that
certificate search exceeds the available budget.  \cref{app:exhaust}
reports the measured growth in candidate-count and wall-clock from
$n = 5$ through $n = 9$, from which the $n = 10$ extrapolation follows.

\subsection{The lifted-defect graph}\label{sec:detector-graph}

Fix a candidate good cycle $gc$ on $\mathbf{m}$.  The
\emph{lifted-defect graph} has vertex set
\[
   V_{\mathrm{lift}}(gc) \,=\, \bigl\{ (k, q, a) \,:\, k \in \mathrm{Fin}(L),\ q \in \mathrm{Fin}(n),\ a \ne c_k(q),\ c_k[q := a] \in T_{N_1}(gc) \bigr\}
\]
with projection $\pi(k, q, a) = c_k[q := a]$.  Edges are induced by the
firings of the cycle and classified into four types according to the
relative position of the cycle mover at step $k$ and the defect
coordinate $q$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{transport}: the mover at step $k$ is far from $q$ (at
distance $\ge 2$);
\item \textbf{c\_self}: the mover at step $k$ is exactly $q$;
\item \textbf{c\_left}: the mover is at position $q - 1$;
\item \textbf{c\_right}: the mover is at position $q + 1$.
\end{itemize}
The 4-type classification partitions the forward edges of the lifted
graph (verified by the \texttt{classify\_type} audit on $4795$ forward
edges; see \cref{app:detector}).  The 3-type collapse
(transport, c\_sided := c\_left $\cup$ c\_right, c\_self) is direction-covariant.

\subsection{The circulation detector}\label{sec:detector-lp}

For a given good cycle $gc$, set up the linear program
\[
   \text{find }\Phi : E_{\mathrm{lift}} \to \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}
   \quad\text{with}\quad B^\top \Phi = 0
   \quad\text{and}\quad \textstyle\sum_{e} \Phi_e = 1,
\]
where $B$ is the incidence matrix of the lifted-defect graph.  The
positive-mass normalization $\sum_e \Phi_e = 1$ replaces the abstract
$\Phi \ne 0$ condition with a bona-fide linear constraint, so that the
search is an actual linear program rather than a feasibility predicate.
By Hoffman's circulation theorem~\cite{Hoffman1960}, this LP is
feasible if and only if the lifted graph contains a directed cycle.
This step is a theorem.

\begin{remark}[Detector status]\label{rem:detector-status}
The detector \emph{predicts}, empirically, that circulation feasibility
corresponds to obstruction: on every tested record, feasible circulation
appears exactly at sub-threshold candidates and infeasible circulation
appears exactly at valid-system witnesses (sharp or super-threshold).
A proof would require showing that a lifted directed cycle forces
$\peelTube(gc)$ to be nonempty.  The downstream consumers
\texttt{sk\_nonempty\_via\_tube\_\{small,large\}\_n} turn
$\peelTube(gc).\mathrm{Nonempty}$ into $\SK(gc).\mathrm{Nonempty}$, and
\texttt{SinkKernel.not\_converges\_of\_SK\_nonempty} turns
$\SK(gc).\mathrm{Nonempty}$ into nonconvergence; these two steps are
established.  The missing step is
``lifted circulation $\Rightarrow$ $\peelTube(gc).\mathrm{Nonempty}$.''
\end{remark}

\subsection{Detector failures at $n = 9$}\label{sec:detector-n9-counterexamples}

Extending the sub-threshold enumeration to the three product-$7776$
multisets of Table~7 (\S\ref{sec:main-phase}: $\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$,

\item $k_{\mathrm{bin}} = 3$ (three binary processors);
\item \texttt{bin\_adj\_pairs} $= 0$ (no two binary positions are
      cyclically adjacent);
\item $\min$-binary-gap $= 2$ (binary positions are pairwise
      separated by at least one ternary and nowhere consecutive).
\end{itemize}

$\{2^5, 3^3, 9\}$ is listed alongside the other two

C1 detector failing to fire, i.e.\ returning LP-infeasible,

multiset.  Importantly, the structural sub-family on which the
detector fails coincides with the
\emph{non-adjacent binary} zone that ARG's 1985 LCM bound treats
(\S\ref{sec:lower-arg}), and the same sub-family on which ARG's
LCM-functional is constant between $n = 8$ and $n = 9$.  The C1 LP
is silent on the same sub-family as ARG's bound: it is a detector
on every tested record \emph{outside} the non-adjacent $k = 3$
binary zone and is silent inside it.

the supplementary memo listed in \cref{app:manifest}, where a
restricted-to-edge-type construction is marked RED and two
alternative axes (full-forced-NG sink-kernel detector, structural
template-match against the ternary-strip witness) are open.

\textbf{Relation to existing open question.} The restricted-
feasibility fact of \S\ref{sec:detector-tsided}
(\emph{c\_self edges are empirically unnecessary on every tested
$n \le 8$ sub-threshold record}) remains intact: on the five
LP-feasible $n = 9$ records the T+sided-only LP preserves
feasibility with unchanged support size, and on the four
counterexamples both C1 and T+sided-only are infeasible.  The
open question stated in \cref{conj:tsided,sec:open-tsided} updates
to: \emph{every good cycle at
$n \ge 9$ with product below $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ \emph{and} outside
the $k_{\mathrm{bin}} = 3$ non-adjacent-binary sub-family has a
directed cycle in the lifted transport-plus-sided graph; the
sub-family is conjectured to require a distinct LB mechanism.}

\subsection{The Axis C detector for $n \ge 9$}\label{sec:detector-axis-c}

The four $n = 9$ counterexamples of
\cref{sec:detector-n9-counterexamples} are recovered by a strict
upgrade of the circulation detector that leaves the Hamming-$1$
tube: the \emph{sink kernel of the candidate's forced-NG graph}.
The upgrade is machine-rechecked on the full $97$-record canonical
corpus artifact (overall hash \texttt{f4b017b1f57687cc}; archived
with the repository bundle of \cref{app:exhaust-artifact}); the
$72$-record slice previously shipped covers $4$ at\_smallN
absorbers $+\;59$ $n = 8$ sub-threshold $+\;9$ $n = 9$ Table-7
sub-threshold records, with the remaining $25$ ($19$ sub-threshold
at $n \in \{5, 6, 7\}$ $+\;6$ at\_clb witnesses at $n \in \{5,
\ldots, 10\}$) filled in by the reconciliation pass documented in
the supplementary memo listed in \cref{app:manifest}.  The memo
consolidating all eight probe axes is also listed in
\cref{app:manifest}.

\paragraph{Definition.} Given a candidate record $(\mathbf{m},
\mathrm{cycle}, \mathrm{movers}, \detOf)$ let $\mathrm{NG} =
\prod_i \mathbb{Z}_{m_i} \setminus \mathrm{cycle}$ be the non-good
configurations and let the \emph{forced-NG digraph} on $\mathrm{NG}$
have an edge $c \to c'$ whenever some processor $p$ has
$\detOf[(p, c_{p-1}, c_p, c_{p+1})] \ne c_p$ and applying that move
yields $c' \in \mathrm{NG}$.  The \emph{sink kernel} is the largest
$S \subseteq \mathrm{NG}$ such that every $c \in S$ has at least
one out-edge into $S$ (equivalently, the fixed point of iterative
removal of vertices with no out-neighbor in the current set).
The Axis C detector \emph{fires} on a record iff its sink kernel is
nonempty.

\paragraph{Monotonicity certificate.}  $\SK$ is defined by a
coclosure-style fixed point, it is the largest subset $S$ of
$\mathrm{NG}$ in which every vertex has at least one out-neighbor
in $S$.  Adding forced edges can only add out-neighbors to a given
vertex; it cannot remove any.  Consequently, every vertex that
qualified for membership in $S$ under the smaller edge set still
qualifies under the larger one, and $\SK$ grows monotonically in
the edge set (equivalently, in $\detOf$): adding forced edges can
only enlarge $\SK$.  Every extension of the partial $\detOf$ to a
full transition function $f$ adds forced edges, so
$\SK(f) \supseteq \SK(\detOf)$.
A valid self-stabilizing system has $\SK(f) = \emptyset$ under its
full $f$ (convergence forbids any closed set of non-good configs
under forced moves).  Hence:
\emph{$\SK(\detOf) \ne \emptyset$ on a candidate record $\Rightarrow$
no extension of $\detOf$ to a valid system exists.}  This is the
one-sided sub-threshold certificate the circulation LP provides for
$n \le 8$, now extended past the $n = 9$ sub-family on which C1
was silent.

\paragraph{Corpus verdict.}  Axis C fires on all $87$ sub-threshold
records of the $97$-record canonical corpus (including all $4$
$n = 9$ counterexamples of \cref{sec:detector-n9-counterexamples})
and is silent on all $10$ valid witnesses ($4$ small-$n$ absorbers
at $n \in \{5, \ldots, 8\}$ plus $6$ at\_clb ternary-strip witnesses
at $n \in \{5, \ldots, 10\}$): separation is $97/97$ overall.  The
$87$ sub-threshold count decomposes as $19$ records at
$n \in \{5, 6, 7\}$, $59$ at $n = 8$, and $9$ at $n = 9$ (Table 7);
the $68$-record sub-threshold slice sometimes cited separately is
the $59 + 9$ portion at $n \in \{8, 9\}$.  On the four $n = 9$ counterexamples
$|\SK| / |\mathrm{NG}|$ lies in $[0.26, 0.50]$ with nontrivial
strongly-connected-component counts $\{12, 16, 38, 12\}$ and
largest-SCC sizes $\{1020, 1050, 1185, 930\}$; the peel geometry is
substantial and is the concrete object targeted by the analytical
open problem below.

\paragraph{Cross-check: the verdict is genuine, not a det-coverage
artifact.}  Before adopting Axis C we audited it against a
potential artifact: the small-$n$ absorber records carry a full
$\detOf$ dictionary while the $n = 9$ sub-threshold records carry
only cycle-visited triples (typical coverage $\sim 0.6\%$).  The
concern was that $|\SK| = 0$ on the absorber records might merely
reflect full-coverage forcing, not a structural property.  Stripping
each absorber's $\detOf$ down to exactly the cycle-visited triples
and recomputing gives $|\SK| = 0$ on all four (with $168$, $480$,
$1812$, $6876$ stripped triples respectively).  The signal is
therefore structural, not coverage-driven; the sibling axis D
(identity-completion verifier) fires $4/4$ but was traced to the
coverage asymmetry and is retired.

\paragraph{Relation to the circulation LP.}  The C1 circulation LP
of \cref{sec:detector-lp} is a strictly weaker detector: on the
$97$-record canonical corpus, $\text{C1 feasible} \Rightarrow
\text{Axis C fires}$ holds with zero counterexamples, and the four
$n = 9$ records in $\text{Axis C} \setminus \text{C1}$ are exactly
the non-adjacent $\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$ orderings.  Structurally: a
feasible C1 gives a non-trivial non-negative circulation on the
Hamming-$1$ tube, and that circulation lifts to a directed cycle
in the forced-NG graph at Hamming distance $\le 1$ from the good
cycle; Axis C allows forced cycles at any Hamming distance, which
is where the four $n = 9$ counterexamples live.

\paragraph{Analytical status.}  Axis C's detector verdict on the
corpus is machine-recheckable at $O(|\mathrm{NG}|)$ per record (no
LP solve); the structural certificate (sink-kernel monotonicity) is
proved.  What remains open is the \emph{uniform} statement:
whether $\SK(\detOf) \ne \emptyset$ holds on \emph{every}
sub-threshold candidate record at arbitrary $n$, not just the $87$
sub-threshold records in the canonical corpus.  This is a cleaner target than the C1
``feasible $\Rightarrow$ $\peelTube$-nonempty'' implication:
Axis C is not restricted to the Hamming-$1$ tube, and the
sink-kernel is a discrete topological invariant of
$X(\mathbf{m}) \setminus \mathrm{cycle}$ (a closure property of the
forced-move digraph, not an arithmetic one on $\detOf$).  It is the
``topological invariant forbids it''-shaped obligation the
analytical program has been trying to isolate.

\paragraph{Extended $n = 10$ sub-threshold sweep.}  The $87$
sub-threshold records of the canonical corpus above cover
$n \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$; $n = 10$ is absent because the
small-$n$ probe budget used for the canonical corpus does not
reach $n = 10$ cycle enumeration.  A separate full-$n = 10$ sweep
probes every one of the $291$ sorted (``canonical'') sub-threshold
$n = 10$ multisets using two orthogonal tools: (a) a DFS good-cycle
enumerator (\texttt{probe\_axisc\_n10\_sweep.py}, $30\,\mathrm{s}$
budget per canonical ordering, $2$ orderings per multiset) and
(b) a z3 SMT cycle-existence encoder
(\texttt{probe\_cycle\_smt.py} / \texttt{sweep\_smt\_n10\_uncovered.py},
a first-order encoding of $\mathrm{state}[t, p]$ and
$\mathrm{mover}[t]$ variables with transition, fairness, closure,
and det-consistency constraints) run on every multiset the DFS
enumerator could not find a cycle for within its budget.  The SMT
encoding's cost is polynomial in the cycle length and independent
of $\prod m$, so it closes multisets on which brute-force DFS
exceeds its $30\,\mathrm{s}$ cap.  Combining the two tools:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item DFS found good cycles on $77$ multisets and ran Axis C on
every cycle;
\item SMT found good cycles on the remaining $214$ multisets and
ran Axis C on every cycle;
\item across $\mathbf{320}$ total
$(\mathrm{multiset}, \mathrm{ordering})$ probes covering every one
of the $291$ sub-threshold multisets,
\emph{Axis C fires on every probe, zero silent}
($\mathbf{320 / 320}$).
\end{itemize}
Sink-kernel geometry across the probes: $|\SK|/|\mathrm{NG}|$ in
$[0.038, 1.000]$, mean $0.175$; good-cycle length $L$ in
$[20, 59]$.

\paragraph{Extended $n = 11$ sub-threshold sweep.}  The same
two-tool pipeline extends to $n = 11$: the DFS enumerator on all
$564$ sorted sub-threshold multisets (same $30\,\mathrm{s}$-per-ordering
budget, $2$ orderings per multiset) surfaces good cycles on $90$
multisets across $112$ probes, and the SMT gap-closer
(\texttt{sweep\_smt\_n10\_uncovered.py} reused with
\texttt{--L-max 30}, $90\,\mathrm{s}$ timeout per multiset) closes
$473$ of the remaining $474$ budget-limited multisets.  Combining:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item DFS found good cycles on $90$ multisets and ran Axis~C on
every cycle;
\item SMT found good cycles on $473$ of the $474$ DFS-unreached
multisets and ran Axis~C on every cycle;
\item $1$ multiset ($[2^8, 4, 4, 6]$) returned UNKNOWN at the
SMT timeout — a solver-budget outcome, not evidence of an obstruction;
\item across $\mathbf{585}$ total
$(\mathrm{multiset}, \mathrm{ordering})$ probes covering $563$ of
the $564$ sub-threshold multisets,
\emph{Axis~C fires on every probe, zero silent}
($\mathbf{585 / 585}$).
\end{itemize}
Sink-kernel geometry across the probes: $|\SK|/|\mathrm{NG}|$ in
$[0.026, 0.999]$, mean $0.110$; good-cycle length $L$ in
$[22, 65]$.  This extends the $n = 10$ verdict one layer further
and is cleanly consistent with the uniform claim
\cref{conj:ub-allN,conj:lb-allN} predicts at $n = 11$.  Artifacts:
\texttt{axc\_n11\_sweep\_results.json} (DFS sweep),
\texttt{axc\_n11\_smt\_sweep\_results.json} (SMT-closed sweep).

\paragraph{Scope caveat.}  Axis~C monotonicity eliminates a
specific $(\mathrm{cycle}, \detOf)$ candidate, not a whole
multiset: a multiset is ruled out only up to the $(\mathrm{cycle},
\mathrm{ordering}, \detOf)$ probes actually surfaced by the
pipeline.  The DFS half uses a fixed $30\,\mathrm{s}$ budget and
two canonical orderings per multiset; the SMT half finds \emph{a}
good cycle per multiset, not all of them.  ``$320 / 320$'' at $n
= 10$ and ``$585 / 585$'' at $n = 11$ therefore mean \emph{every
probe that the two-tool pipeline surfaced fires Axis~C}, not
\emph{every candidate good cycle on every multiset does}.
Completeness of the good-cycle enumerator under the search's
pruning discipline is the additional gap between a per-probe
statement and a per-multiset statement; it coincides with the
exhaustive-search completeness question of \cref{app:exhaust} and
is not separately claimed here.  This is the sense in which
\cref{conj:lb-allN} is ``Axis~C-supported'' at $n = 10, 11$:
supported per probe, not proved per multiset.  The verdict is
nevertheless cleanly consistent with the uniform claim
\cref{conj:ub-allN,conj:lb-allN} predicts at $n = 10, 11$.  The
$n = 10$ DFS sweep ($291$ multisets in $3.7\,\mathrm{h}$), its
SMT-closed sweep ($214$ budget-limited multisets, $2.8\,\mathrm{h}$),
the $n = 11$ DFS sweep ($564$ multisets), its SMT-closed sweep
($473$ of $474$ budget-limited multisets), and the combined
findings memo are all archived in the repository bundle
(\cref{app:exhaust-artifact}) and indexed in \cref{app:manifest}.

\subsection{Supplementary probes}\label{sec:detector-supplementary}

\textbf{Sharpness probe.} At $n \in \{5, 6\}$, feasibility transitions
sharply at $\prod m = \Mn$: every immediately-sub-threshold candidate
is feasible and every $\Mn$-threshold valid witness is infeasible.
At $n = 7$ sub-side cycle enumeration timed out; this caveat is noted.

\textbf{Random-multiset probe.} $15$ random sub-threshold multisets at
$n \in \{5, 6, 7\}$; for the $12$ that admitted a candidate good cycle,
all $12$ were LP-feasible.  The detector generalizes beyond the
\texttt{enumerate\_multisets} stratification.

\textbf{Perturbation probe.} $11$ edge-definition and orientation
variations were tested; no record flipped verdict.  The route is
robust to edge-definition perturbation.

\subsection{The restricted-feasibility observation}\label{sec:detector-tsided}

\textbf{Primary observation (empirical).} \emph{After forbidding c\_self
edges entirely, circulation remains feasible on all $19$ sub-threshold
records.}  This is the central observation of the subsection.

\textbf{Secondary observation (selected LP optimum).} On the LP optima
returned by the default solver, c\_self carries zero flow on all $19$
records.  This is a statement about a selected optimum; if the LP optimum
is non-unique, some alternate optimum may place positive flow on c\_self.
The restricted-feasibility statement above does not depend on optimum
selection.

\textbf{Edge-type flow statistics (selected optima).}
On the orientation-invariant 3-type collapse, the selected-optimum
mean support is transport $66.5\%$, c\_sided $33.5\%$, c\_self $0\%$
(the split of c\_sided into c\_right $33.2\%$ and c\_left $0.3\%$ is
a cycle-orientation artifact: reversing the cycle swaps the two, so
only the sum is a property of the record).  The c\_self edges
constitute up to $14.9\%$ of the lifted graph but contribute $0\%$ in
the selected optima.

On the tested corpus, the c\_self edges are empirically unnecessary for
circulation; forbidding them preserves feasibility on every sub-threshold
record tested.  This suggests that any eventual proof may be able to
work entirely in the transport-plus-sided subgraph.

\textbf{Analytical status.}  The proof attempt, a $q$-tube forward
walk in the transport-plus-sided subgraph, stalls at a specific
named obstruction.  The walk advances cleanly on transport and
sided-adjacent steps but hits a ``hole'' at every step where the cycle
mover equals the defect coordinate ($\mathrm{mov}_k = q$).  Every
position $q$ fires at least once per good cycle (fairness), so every
$q$-tube walk has at least one hole.  Closing the hole requires
cycle-local $\detOf(gc)$ information.  This obstruction is one of the
five families catalogued in \cref{sec:landscape}.

\subsection{What this section establishes (and what it does not)}\label{sec:detector-summary}

\Cref{sec:detector} establishes:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item separation of all $97$ records of the extended corpus at
$n \in \{5, \ldots, 10\}$ under the Axis C sink-kernel detector
(\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}), and separation of $93$ of $97$ under
the circulation-LP detector of \cref{sec:detector-lp}, the four
C1-infeasible $n = 9$ sub-threshold records of
\cref{sec:detector-n9-counterexamples} being exactly
$\text{Axis C} \setminus \text{C1}$;
\item robustness of C1 to edge-definition perturbation and random sub-threshold sampling;
\item restricted feasibility without c\_self on every tested sub-threshold record;
\item a one-sided sub-threshold certificate via sink-kernel monotonicity
on the forced-NG digraph (\cref{sec:detector-axis-c});
\item a concrete obstruction mechanism pointing at the
$(C, \mu) \times \detOf$ interaction.
\end{itemize}
It \emph{does not} establish:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item any extension of the corpus-separation claim beyond the $97$
records or beyond $n \le 10$;
\item a uniform-in-$n$ proof that $\SK(\detOf) \ne \emptyset$ on every
sub-threshold candidate (\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}), nor a proof of
the analogous C1 implication ``feasible $\Rightarrow$ $\peelTube$-nonempty.''
\end{itemize}

The specific statement is \cref{conj:tsided} of
\cref{sec:open-tsided}; empirical support is substantial but
analytical attempts fail at mover-equals-defect steps
(\cref{sec:landscape-flow}).

\section{A map of the obstruction landscape}\label{sec:landscape}
% =====================================================================

This section catalogs systematic analytical attempts at the lower
bound, organized by the underlying invariant family.  Five families
were tested.  Each subsection follows the same five-point template:
proposed invariant, target lemma, what was tested, failure mode, what
this teaches a future attempt.  Long-form descriptions of each tested
approach are deferred to \cref{app:landscape-detail}.

\subsection{Framing}\label{sec:landscape-framing}

The five families are: flow-theoretic decomposition; ambient
topological invariants; map-level invariants; sheaf cohomology; and
arithmetic extraction.  We treat each in turn.

\subsection{Family 1: flow-theoretic decomposition}\label{sec:landscape-flow}

\textbf{Proposed invariant.} A per-vertex balance identity on the
$4$-type decomposition (transport, c\_self, c\_left, c\_right), or in
the direction-covariant restatement on the $3$-type decomposition
(transport, c\_sided, c\_self).

\textbf{Target lemma.} For every good cycle at $n \ge 9$ with product
below $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ and every defect tube indexed by $q$,
transport-plus-sided edges close a directed walk in the lifted graph.

\textbf{What was tested.} The $4$-type per-vertex balance
identity~\cite{HaddadKnuth1985} (failed, $4.9\%$ leading-order
residual against a $1\%$ discrimination target); the
direction-covariant $3$-type balance (failed, $2.7\%$ residual); two
analytical proof attempts of T+sided-only directed-cycle existence
(\cref{sec:detector-tsided} and \cref{app:landscape-detail}).

\textbf{Failure mode.} The $q$-tube forward walk at a lifted vertex
$(k, q, a)$ advances cleanly on transport and sided-adjacent steps but
hits a \emph{mover-equals-defect hole} at every step where
$\mathrm{mov}_k = q$.  Fairness guarantees every $q$ encounters such a
step.  Closing the hole requires cycle-local $\detOf(gc)$ information,
preventing a uniform-in-$n$ argument.  Two independent attempts identify
the same wall.

\textbf{What this teaches.} The obstruction is localized at
mover-equals-defect steps.  Progress requires either (a) a structural
handle on the move-entry context at these steps that does not
case-split, or (b) a proof of T+sided cycle existence that bypasses the
walk construction entirely (\cref{sec:open-tsided}).

\subsection{Family 2: ambient topological invariants}\label{sec:landscape-ambient}

\textbf{Proposed invariants.} $\pi_1$ and $H_1$ of $\NGset(C)$; the
linking matrix of mover-class subcycles; Lefschetz numbers and
Artin--Mazur $\zeta_f$; Cheeger and spectral-gap of the forced
neighborhood graph; Forman--Ricci curvature; discrete Morse theory.

\textbf{What they would need to prove.} A homotopy- or curvature-level
invariant that distinguishes sub-threshold good cycles from
valid-system good cycles.

\textbf{What was tested.} See \cref{app:landscape-detail}.

\textbf{Failure mode.} All tested invariants scale with ambient
dimension $(n, L)$ rather than with per-$m_i$ information.  $\pi_1 = 0$
on tested records; the linking matrix is circulant, forcing
$\det \Lambda = 0$; Lefschetz is trivial by contractibility; Cheeger
$\lambda_2 = 0$ (graph disconnected in both classes); Forman--Ricci is
monotone in $|V|$; the Conley-style $\beta_1 / |N|$ ranges
$[1.087, 1.500]$ at sub-threshold and $[1.080, 1.500]$ at threshold
fully overlap.  The homotopy type of $\NGset(C) \subseteq \prod \Delta^{m_i - 1}$
is $(n, L)$-parametrized; $\mathbf{m}$ enters only via ambient-cell
counts.

\textbf{What this teaches.} Ambient-space topology is
$(n, L)$-parametrized.  Any future invariant must see $\mathbf{m}$
beyond ambient dimension, and must address the
\emph{coverage-inversion} diagnostic: $|\detOf| / T_{\mathrm{total}}
\approx L / (n \cdot \prod m)$ \emph{decreases} as the product grows,
pushing any monotone-in-coverage invariant in the wrong direction for
a lower bound.

\subsection{Family 3: map-level invariants}\label{sec:landscape-map}

\textbf{Proposed invariant.} The Conley index of the good cycle as an
isolated invariant set of the transition map.

\textbf{Target.} An intrinsic dynamical invariant of the system that
distinguishes sub-threshold from valid.

\textbf{What was tested.} Scale-normalized $(\beta_0, \beta_1) / |N|$
pairs of $(N, L)$ where $N = C \cup N_1(C)$ and $L$ is the exit set.

\textbf{Failure mode.} The Conley index is homotopy-invariant under
$f$-homotopies, but at sub-threshold no valid $f$ exists.  Any
completion of the rule table (e.g.\ stay-completion) is arbitrary; the
computed index reflects completion choice, not intrinsic dynamics.
Normalized ranges fully overlap valid-system records.

\textbf{What this teaches.} Standard dynamical-systems invariants that
assume the self-map exists cannot directly transfer.  A map-level
approach would need to treat the ``no valid $f$'' case explicitly,
a pre-dynamical analog, a partial-function Conley framework, or a
relation-valued model (\cref{sec:open-conley}).

\subsection{Family 4: sheaf cohomology}\label{sec:landscape-sheaf}

\textbf{Proposed invariant.} \v{C}ech $H^1$ of an extension-problem
sheaf on the $3$-cells of $\prod \Delta^{m_i - 1}$.

\textbf{Target.} $H^1 \ne 0$ at every sub-threshold good cycle and
$H^1 = 0$ at every valid-system good cycle.

\textbf{What was tested.} The standard sheaf (with restriction maps
the natural projection); the single-priv-propagation sheaf; the
path / cycle-space sheaf on the lifted forced-neighborhood
$1$-skeleton; the convergence-depth-parameterized sheaf.

\textbf{Failure mode.} The standard sheaf's restriction maps on shared
$3$-cells are automatic, reducing \v{C}ech cohomology to the nerve
cohomology with constant coefficients; empirically the nerve
$\beta_1$ ranges over $[27,\, 11179]$ at valid-system records, so
$H^1 \ne 0$ fails to vanish where the conjectured separator would
require it to.  The
three non-standard variants either compute $H^1 = 0$ uniformly, have
overlapping ranges across the two classes, or invert the signal.

\textbf{What this teaches.} The obstruction is global (requires
convergence information), but local-cellular sheaves are by
construction local.  A sheaf formulation would need restriction maps
that encode global structure, e.g.\ convergence-propagation or
single-priv-violation-propagation that is genuinely non-local
(\cref{sec:open-sheaf}).

\subsection{Family 5: arithmetic extraction}\label{sec:landscape-arith}

\textbf{Proposed invariant.} A scalar arithmetic inequality on
$\mathbf{m}$-features that separates sub-threshold from valid.

\textbf{Target.} Provable independence from $\prod m < \Bn$, i.e.\ a
non-tautological separator.

\textbf{What was tested.} A 6-feature linear regression
($R^2 = 0.809$, threshold accuracy $1.0$ on $25$ records); a 10-feature
extension ($R^2 = 0.789$, in-sample accuracy $0.966$, leave-one-out
accuracy $0.931$).

\textbf{Failure mode.} The best approximation is
$\log \Bn(n) - \log \prod m$, the threshold condition itself.  The
extraction is tautological.  An additional ``$n_{\mathrm{bin}} \ge 3$
is the only separator'' reading is falsified by the decisive test
on the small-$n$ absorber witnesses, which are themselves $3$-binary
and infeasible.

\textbf{What this teaches.} A viable arithmetic inequality must be
provably independent of $\prod m < \Bn$; the regression feature spaces
tested contain no such feature.  Future arithmetic attempts must
pre-commit a Newness check against the problem's own definition.

\subsection{Unifying observation}\label{sec:landscape-unify}

\begin{observation}\label{obs:locus}
The failed approaches agree on one point: the obstruction is not
visible in the ambient state space alone.  It lives in the triple
interaction among the good cycle $C$, the mover sequence $\mu$, and
the determined partial rule table $\detOf(gc)$.
\end{observation}

Each family fails for the same reason from a different angle:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item Flow decomposition fails because the interaction appears at
mover-equals-defect steps.
\item Ambient topology fails because it does not see the interaction
at all.
\item Map-level fails because the interaction is pre-dynamical (no
self-map exists).
\item Sheaves fail because the interaction is global, not
local-cellular.
\item Arithmetic fails because the only separator is the threshold
itself.
\end{itemize}
The $(C, \mu) \times \detOf$ interaction is named but not captured by
any standard invariant class tested.

\subsection{Currency reframing test}\label{sec:landscape-currency}

A separate question is whether the failure cascade of
\cref{sec:landscape-flow,sec:landscape-ambient,sec:landscape-map,sec:landscape-sheaf,sec:landscape-arith}
reflects a wrong choice of \emph{currency}: we ask for a lower
bound on $\prod m_i$, but $\prod m_i$ counts the ambient
configuration space and does not directly measure what the
dynamics constrains.  A natural alternative is to measure on the
cycle $C$ itself: the mover-pattern period $T_\mu$, the count
$\kappa$ of $\detOf$-determined entries pinned by $C$, or the
empirical entropy $H$ of the cycle's value distribution.  If any
such quantity $Q(\text{system})$ separates the corpus more cleanly
than $\prod m_i$ does, then the right form of the lower bound is
$Q \ge q(n)$ and the product bound follows by counting.

We tested this on the $97$-record stratified corpus of
\cref{sec:detector-corpus} ($10$ valid witnesses + $87$
sub-threshold candidates).  For each record we computed $T_\mu$,
$\kappa_{\text{mover}}$, $\kappa_{\text{total}}$,
$H_{\text{pos}}$, $H_{\text{joint}}$, and their natural
normalizations.  The summary (\cref{tab:currency-reframe}) is
unambiguous.

\paragraph{Caveat on the saturation column.}  The ``saturation
CV'' comparison on $\log_2 \prod m_i$ is tautological: the $10$
valid records were selected because they realize the conjectured
saturating formula $\Mn = 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$, so of course they lie
on a $\log_2 \prod m / n \approx 1.41$ curve.  The $0.030$ CV there
is a definition, not a discovery, and the CV comparison is included
only to show that no dynamical currency achieves tautological
saturation on the valid side either.  The substantive content of
the table is in the AUC and strict-$n$ columns, and in the
direction flip discussed below.

\paragraph{The real content: direction flip at $n = 9$.}
Independently of the saturation-CV column, each dynamical currency
\emph{reverses direction} at $n = 9$.  The valid record (CLB strip
on $(2, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 3, 2)$) has mover-pattern period
$T_\mu = 3n - 2 = 25$, $\kappa_{\text{mover}} = 25$,
$H_{\text{pos\,sum}} = 12.08$ (with good-configuration count
$L = n^2 - 2n + 8 = 71$).  The nine sub-threshold Table~7 records
have $T_\mu \in \{39, \ldots, 53\}$,
$\kappa_{\text{mover}} \in \{39, \ldots, 47\}$,
$H_{\text{pos\,sum}}$ up to $12.53$.  Each dynamical currency we
report, $T_\mu$, $\kappa$, and $H$, \emph{reverses
direction}: on these measures the sub-threshold cycles are
strictly longer, more mover-active, and more entropic than the
valid one.  Any attempt to lower-bound some monotone function of
mover-pattern period, $\detOf$-coverage, or entropy would flip
the inequality at $n = 9$.  Only $\prod m$ sees that these records
are sub-threshold.

\begin{observation}[Currency null]\label{obs:currency-null}
On the $97$-record stratified corpus, no candidate dynamical
currency $Q \in \{T_\mu, \kappa, H, \text{their ratios}\}$
reorders valid vs.\ sub-threshold more cleanly than $\prod m_i$
does (same or worse AUC, same or worse strict-$n$ count).  More
substantively,
at $n = 9$ every candidate dynamical currency \emph{reverses
direction}; this is the statement with content (the AUC and
saturation-CV columns are ancillary, the latter partly tautological
by the saturating-family selection of the valid side).  The
direction flip rules out the reformulation ``lower-bound a
different, dynamical quantity'': no monotone function of
$T_\mu$, $\kappa$, or $H$ can lower-bound the sub-threshold side
without flipping sign at $n = 9$.  $\prod m_i$ is therefore the right
ambient
coordinate for the obstruction; the obstruction itself remains
located in the joint $(C, \mu) \times \detOf$ interaction
(\cref{obs:locus}).
\end{observation}

This rules out the obvious reformulation, ``solve the lower-bound
problem on a different quantity.''  It does not rule out routes that
extract a structural invariant of $X(\mathbf{m}) \setminus C$ while
keeping $\prod m_i$ as the ambient coordinate; such routes are still
open and indeed match the topological-invariant proof shape we find
most natural for the problem.

\section{Open questions}\label{sec:open}

\subsection{Analytical proof of T+sided-only circulation existence}\label{sec:open-tsided}

\begin{conjecture}[T+sided directed cycle]\label{conj:tsided}
For every good cycle at $n \ge 9$ with product below $4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$,
the subgraph of the lifted forced-NG graph induced by transport
$\cup$ c\_left $\cup$ c\_right contains a directed cycle.
\end{conjecture}
A small-$n$ piecewise variant can be stated for $n \in \{5, \ldots, 8\}$
at product below $32 \cdot 3^{n-4}$.  If \cref{conj:tsided} is proved
together with the missing implication from a lifted directed cycle to
$\peelTube$-nonemptiness, the existing
\texttt{SinkKernel.not\_converges\_of\_SK\_nonempty} machinery yields
the conjectured large-$n$ lower bound.  The two implications are
logically independent; both are currently open.  The specific structural
obstruction is the $q$-tube hole at mover-equals-defect steps
(\cref{sec:landscape-flow}).

\subsection{Uniform Axis C: sink-kernel nonemptiness on every sub-threshold candidate}\label{sec:open-axis-c}

\begin{conjecture}[Uniform Axis C]\label{conj:axis-c}
For every candidate good cycle + partial-$\detOf$ record
$(\mathbf{m}, \mathrm{cycle}, \mathrm{movers}, \detOf)$ produced by
the enumeration pipeline at $n \ge 9$ with $\prod m_i < 4 \cdot
3^{n-2}$, the sink kernel $\SK(\detOf)$ of the forced-NG digraph
(\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}) is nonempty.
\end{conjecture}

By \cref{sec:detector-axis-c}, a proof of \cref{conj:axis-c} would
upgrade the one-sided certificate from the corpus-level empirical
statement to a uniform-in-$n$ structural theorem, discharging the
large-$n$ lower bound without the two-step
(T+sided-only $\Rightarrow$ $\peelTube$) routing.  Axis C is not
restricted to the Hamming-$1$ tube, and the SK operator is a
discrete topological invariant of $X(\mathbf{m}) \setminus
\mathrm{cycle}$ (a closure property of the forced-move digraph), so
the obligation is shaped as ``a topological invariant forbids it''
rather than an arithmetic statement on $\detOf$.  Supporting
evidence at the first $n$ beyond the exhaustive-certificate reach:
the extended $n = 10, 11$ sweeps of \cref{sec:detector-axis-c}
fire Axis C on every one of the $291$ sorted $n = 10$ sub-threshold
multisets ($320$ probes, zero silent) and on $563$ of $564$
sorted $n = 11$ sub-threshold multisets ($585$ probes, zero silent,
one SMT-budget UNKNOWN) across a two-tool (DFS $+$ SMT) pipeline,
extending the canonical-corpus verdict two levels past the
exhaustive certificate at $n = 9$.  Concrete openings flagged by the corpus audit: the four
$n = 9$ non-adjacent $\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$ records have nontrivial-SCC
counts in $\{12, 16, 38, 12\}$ with largest SCCs of size
$\sim 1000$, small enough for a direct analysis of their
forced-orbit structure.

\subsection{A pre-dynamical analog of Conley index}\label{sec:open-conley}

Does a map-level invariant exist that is well-defined when no valid
self-map $f$ exists?  Candidate frameworks: partial-function Conley
index; relation-valued homotopy.  Such an invariant would discriminate
self-stabilizability without requiring the system to be defined.

\subsection{A sheaf formulation encoding global constraints}\label{sec:open-sheaf}

Construct a sheaf whose restriction maps encode
convergence-propagation or single-priv-violation-propagation
non-locally, and compute its $H^1$ on the $97$-record canonical
corpus.  The standard local-cellular design space failed
empirically (\cref{sec:landscape-sheaf}); a non-local sheaf could
in principle access the global obstruction.

\subsection{Extension of empirical verification}\label{sec:open-extend}

Extend the CLB or CUP-2 witness verification beyond $n = 18$, ideally
by replacing finite verification with an all-$n$ symbolic proof that
both rule tables satisfy the six validity properties for every
$n \ge n_0$.
This would promote \cref{conj:ub-allN} from a Python-verified finite
range to an all-$n$ theorem.

\subsection{Lean formalization of the lower bound}\label{sec:open-leanlb}

Prove $\Mn \ge 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ in Lean 4 / Mathlib for some $n \ge 10$,
or for a restricted subclass of good cycles (e.g.\ those with
$\mathrm{longest\_ter\_run} \le 1$).  This would move part of the
lower-bound program from computational evidence toward
proof-assistant-certified mathematics.

\subsection{Relationship to ARG's 1985 binary-count conjecture}\label{sec:open-arg}

In what restricted settings does \cref{conj:lb-allN} imply ARG's
binary-count conjecture (no valid system has more than a constant
number of binary processors as $n \to \infty$)?  At ternary-dense
$\{2, 3\}$ multisets the implication is immediate: \cref{conj:lb-allN}
forces $2^k \cdot 3^{n-k} \ge 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$, hence $k \le 2$.
With higher-state compensation allowed the implication is not
immediate.

\subsection{Transport to the relaxed-connectedness convention}\label{sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}

All bounds of \cref{sec:main} are stated in the Dijkstra-connected
model of \cref{sec:prelim-model}.  The 1985 Stanford seminar worked
under a relaxation that admitted multi-good-cycle constructions
(including the $n = 4$ Gray-code family at product $16$); write
$\Mn^{\mathrm{rel}}$ for the minimum state product under that
convention.  The first-data result $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_5 = 96$
was stated in \cref{sec:main-relaxed} (\cref{thm:m5rel}); this
subsection records the underlying SMT table and the open questions
beyond $n = 5$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item Is $\Mn^{\mathrm{rel}} = \Mn$ for every $n \ge 5$?
\item For $n \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$, is $\Mn^{\mathrm{rel}}$ equal
to the value in \cref{thm:exact-values}, or strictly smaller
(as it is at $n = 4$, where $M_4^{\mathrm{rel}} \le 16 < 24 = M_4$)?
\item Does the conjecture $\Mn = 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ of
\cref{conj:ub-allN,conj:lb-allN} transport to $\Mn^{\mathrm{rel}}$,
and hence resolve Knuth's original 1985 questions as stated?
\end{enumerate}
A positive answer to (1) would make the connected-model bounds of
this paper directly applicable to Knuth's questions as originally
stated; until then, the resolution offered by
\cref{cor:asymptotic} is of the connected-variant questions only.
\Cref{thm:m5rel} answers question (1) positively at $n = 5$ and
question (2) negatively at $n = 5$.

\paragraph{First data (randomized search).}  A standalone relaxed
verifier (shipped with the repository bundle) confirms that every
stored connected-valid witness ($w_{4\mathrm{opt}}, w_5, w_6, w_7,
w_8$) is also relaxed-valid (relaxed $\supseteq$ connected, as
expected).  Brute-force rule enumeration at $n = 5$ is infeasible
(approximately $10^{10}$--$10^{12}$ rule systems per sub-threshold
multiset), but a randomized sub-threshold sample of $5.5 \times
10^6$ rule systems distributed over five sub-target multisets
($\{2^5\}, \{2^4,3\}, \{2^3, 3^2\}$ at $n = 5$; $\{2^6\}, \{2^5, 3\}$
at $n = 6$) yielded zero relaxed-valid hits.  This is not a proof,
but it is first negative data against $M_n^{\mathrm{rel}} < M_n$ at
$n \in \{5, 6\}$.

\paragraph{SMT table for $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_5$.}  A z3 SMT
encoding of the five relaxed-convention validity properties (with a
\texttt{--sym-break} flag enabling WLOG quotients under the
per-processor value-swap action and cyclic rotation of processor
indices on $(\mathrm{good}, f)$) certifies that \emph{no
relaxed-valid rule table exists at any of the five $n = 5$
sub-threshold multisets}:

\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{l r r l r}
\toprule
multiset     & $\prod m$ & $M_5$ & verdict & z3 time\\\midrule
$\{2^5\}$       &  $32$ & $96$ & UNSAT &  $0.02\,\mathrm{s}$\\
$\{2^4, 3\}$    &  $48$ & $96$ & UNSAT &  $0.14\,\mathrm{s}$\\
$\{2^4, 4\}$    &  $64$ & $96$ & UNSAT &  $8.94\,\mathrm{s}$\\

Total sub-threshold runtime $\approx 130\,\mathrm{s}$.  An

correct.  Combined, these certificates give $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} \ge
96$, and since $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} \le M_5 = 96$ is automatic,
$M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_5 = 96$ (\cref{thm:m5rel}).

\paragraph{$n = 6$ partial sweep.}  Of the twelve $n = 6$

\section{Related work}\label{sec:related}
% =====================================================================

\subsection{Classical}\label{sec:related-classical}

Dijkstra~\cite{Dijkstra1974} introduced self-stabilization;
Tchuente~\cite{Tchuente1981} showed that in the uniform case
($m_i = m$ for all $i$) the common state count must satisfy
$m \ge 3$, motivating Knuth's product-minimization generalization; Haddad--Knuth~\cite{HaddadKnuth1985} (STAN-CS-85-1055)
includes the seminar's $\Mn > 2^n$ bound for $n > 4$, ARG's
non-adjacent-binary LCM bound $L \ge N + 1$, and the
quasi-unidirectionality bound; Dijkstra~\cite{Dijkstra1986} gave the
belated proof of Solution 3 correctness.

ARG's LCM bound remains the sharpest 1985-era structural constraint on
the non-adjacent-binary sub-family and underpins both the all-binary
impossibility reproof and the $k \le 4$ binary cap in
$\{2^k, 3^{n-k}\}$.  As discussed in \cref{sec:lower-arg}, the bound
does not extend to the adjacent-binary witnesses that realize $\Mn$ for
$n \in \{4, \ldots, 8\}$.

\subsection{Post-1985}\label{sec:related-post1985}

Burns--Pachl~\cite{BurnsPachl1989} addressed uniform self-stabilizing
rings without a distinguished processor; Ghosh~\cite{Ghosh1993}
established the uniform 3-state lower bound under the central daemon
with deterministic execution; Gouda--Haddix~\cite{GoudaHaddix1996} gave
the first stabilizing unidirectional deterministic token ring with a
constant ($8$-state) per-process state count;
Beauquier--Debas~\cite{BeauquierDebas1995} gave an optimal $3$-state
algorithm for bidirectional non-uniform rings with $O(n^2)$
stabilization time; Hoepman~\cite{Hoepman1999} tightened bounds for
Solution 1; Blin--Le Bouder--Petit~\cite{BlinLeBouderPetit2024}
established the $O(\log \log n)$-bit memory threshold for
self-stabilizing token circulation in a different model;
Protocon~\cite{KlinkhamerEbnenasir2013} performs automated synthesis for the
uniform-state version of the problem.  None of these results addresses
the non-uniform product-minimization question of
\cref{sec:prelim-Mn}.

\subsection{Lean and formal verification in distributed algorithms}\label{sec:related-formal}

Prior formal verification of self-stabilization includes a PVS
treatment of Dijkstra's token ring, Coq frameworks for certified
self-stabilization, and PADEC/Coq certifications of stabilization-time
complexity.  Relative to that prior work, the Lean 4 / Mathlib
formalization underlying
\cref{thm:upper-bound-cert,thm:upper-bound-small,thm:M4-eq-24} appears
to be the first Lean 4 formalization of non-uniform
state-product witnesses for Dijkstra's token-ring product-minimization
problem at the scale considered here (state-count product up to
$26244$, ring size up to $10$, with exhaustive per-$n$ rank-table
convergence certificates).  The stated Lean theorems are
sorry-free; finite per-$n$ certificate tables are discharged by
\texttt{native\_decide} on sealed data.

\section{Conclusion}\label{sec:conclusion}
% =====================================================================

We have determined $\Mn$ exactly for $n \in \{3, \ldots, 9\}$
(\cref{thm:exact-values}): the rows $n \in \{3, 4\}$ are
unconditional; the rows $n \in \{5, \ldots, 9\}$ are backed by the
replayable rejection-certificate stream shipped under
\texttt{artifacts/rejections/} in the release tag
(\cref{app:exhaust-artifact}).  We have certified the sharp ratio
crossover at $n = 9$ (\cref{rem:phase-scope}) and stated the
conjecture $\Mn = 4 \cdot 3^{n-2}$ for all $n \ge 9$.  The conjecture
is supported by Lean-checked upper-bound constructions for
$n \in \{4, \ldots, 10\}$, Python-verified constructions through
$n = 18$, an exhaustive lower-bound certificate at $n = 9$, and
structural detectors that separate the stratified corpus at
$n \le 9$. The circulation-LP detector separates every tested
$n \le 8$ record (78 sub-threshold candidates LP-feasible and 8
$n \le 8$ valid witnesses LP-infeasible); at $n = 9$ it fires on $5$
of $9$ tested sub-threshold orderings of the three Table~7 multisets
and returns LP-infeasible on the remaining $4$, which structurally
localize in the non-adjacent $k_{\mathrm{bin}} = 3$ sub-family of
$\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$, the same sub-family on which ARG's 1985 LCM
bound is also silent.  A strict upgrade of the detector,
Axis C (\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}), takes the sink kernel of the
candidate's forced-NG digraph and fires on all $87$ sub-threshold
records of the canonical $97$-record corpus (including the four
C1 blind-spot records) while remaining silent on every valid-witness
record, delivering a one-sided sub-threshold certificate via
monotonicity in $\detOf$.  Extended two-tool (DFS + SMT)
sub-threshold sweeps at $n = 10, 11$ fire Axis~C on every probe
surfaced by the pipeline: $320$ probes across all $291$ sorted
$n = 10$ sub-threshold multisets and $585$ probes across $563$
of $564$ sorted $n = 11$ sub-threshold multisets (one SMT-budget
UNKNOWN), zero silent.  This is a per-probe statement, not a
per-multiset completeness claim, see the scope caveat of
\cref{sec:detector-axis-c}.  Additionally, an SMT certificate
shows $M_5^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_5 = 96$ (\cref{thm:m5rel}), the first
case at $n \ge 5$ in which $M_n^{\mathrm{rel}} = M_n$.  See
\cref{sec:detector-n9-counterexamples,sec:detector-axis-c,sec:main-relaxed}.
We have located the analytical obstruction at the joint interaction
of the good cycle, its mover sequence, and the determined rule-table
entries, and have catalogued five families of failed analytical
attempts on this interaction.

What we do \emph{not} claim: a proof of the uniform lower bound or
a proof of the all-$n$ upper bound.  The mechanism of the $n = 9$
crossover and the transport of our bounds to the
relaxed-connectedness convention beyond $n = 5$ are discussed in
\cref{sec:lower-arg,sec:main-relaxed,sec:open-relaxed-vs-connected}.
The state of the problem after this paper is: the small-$n$ regime
is exact, the crossover is certified, the large-$n$ shape is
conjectured with concrete supporting evidence, and the central
open problem (\cref{conj:tsided}) is named with a specific
structural obstruction.

\subsection{Encoding audit: validity predicate versus prose definition}\label{app:lean-encoding-audit}

Because the stated Lean theorems discharge their finite content
through \texttt{native\_decide} on sealed certificates generated by
auxiliary Python (\texttt{gen\_cup2\_ranks.py} and siblings), the
correctness of the final Lean statement is only as strong as the
correctness of the Lean-level \emph{validity predicate}
\texttt{IsValid} that encodes the six properties of
\cref{sec:prelim-five}.  A bug in \texttt{IsValid} (e.g.\ a
missing clause, a swapped index, an off-by-one in the move relation)
would allow a correctly-computed decision procedure to certify a
mathematically-false theorem.  The audit against this failure mode
has three layers:

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Clause-by-clause transcription audit.} The Lean
\texttt{IsValid} predicate is structured as a conjunction of six
named sub-predicates, one per property of \cref{sec:prelim-five},
and each sub-predicate carries a docstring that quotes the prose
definition of that property verbatim.  An auditor can check
clause-by-clause that each of (1)--(6) is realized in Lean as the
conjunction-named sub-predicate claims to realize.
\item \textbf{Dual-path re-verification.} Every stored witness is
independently re-checked by the Python verifier
(\texttt{verify\_witnesses.py}, \texttt{verifier.py};
\cref{app:manifest}), whose \texttt{verify\_system} routine is a
pure transcription of the six prose properties with no shared data
structures with the Lean side.  A disagreement between the two
paths on any witness (Lean certifies valid, Python rejects; or
vice-versa) is a run-time failure; no such disagreement has been
observed on any stored witness across $n \in \{4, \ldots, 10\}$ or
on the $n = 4$ non-witness $(2, 2, 2, 2)$ (which Python rejects,
Lean rejects via \texttt{M\_4\_eq\_24}'s lower-bound arm).
\item \textbf{Negative-control checks.} The same auto-generated
rank-table machinery was run against deliberately-broken candidate
witnesses (a rule-table entry with the wrong output, a cycle with a
duplicated configuration, a cycle that skips a required firing) as
sanity checks during development; each negative control failed the
Lean \texttt{native\_decide} call at the expected rank-table row.
These logs are archived alongside the certificate stream in
\cref{app:exhaust-artifact}.
\end{enumerate}

The audit trail is shipped as an automated cross-verification script
(\texttt{parity\_runner.py}) with reviewer-facing documentation
indexed in \cref{app:manifest}.  The runner executes all three audit
layers as exit-code-returning steps:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{Layer 1 (transcription).} Parses the Lean \texttt{IsValid}
constituent rule tables (\texttt{wNM}, \texttt{wNP0..wN\_Pk} in
\texttt{LeanMn/SmallN/Defs.lean}; the five universal tables
\texttt{TBotVal, TLowVal, TMidVal, THighVal, TTopVal} in
\texttt{LeanMn/Tables.lean}) and compares them entry-by-entry
against the Python encodings
(\texttt{verify\_witnesses.py::witness\_nN}, for $N \in \{4, \text{4opt}, 5, 6, 7, 8\}$;
the five universal tables of
\texttt{cup2\_theorem.py}).  Expected: zero diffs, with a
SHA-256-truncated \emph{rule-table hash} printed per witness that
matches across encodings.
\item \emph{Layer 2 (dual-path re-verification).} Independently runs
the Python \texttt{verify\_system} on both the Lean-parsed table and
the Python-loaded table; for the CUP-2 family also runs
\texttt{verify\_system} on \texttt{cup2\_theorem.build\_system(n)}
for $n \in \{4, 5, \ldots, 10\}$, confirming validity at every
$n$ in the Lean-formalized range (with mover-pattern period
matching the closed-form $T_\mu = 3n - 2$ and good-configuration
count matching $L = (n+2)(n+3)/2 - 5$).  The \texttt{-{}-lake} flag additionally invokes
\texttt{lake build LeanMn.SmallN.Defs}, whose success certifies that
every \texttt{wN\_valid} theorem still type-checks against the current
Lean+Mathlib stack.
\item \emph{Layer 3 (negative controls).} For each stored witness, the
runner mutates a single move-entry in a copy of the Lean-parsed rule

\texttt{valid=False} on every mutated copy.
\end{itemize}
A referee can rerun the cross-verification with a single command

\section{The circulation detector: formal construction}\label{app:detector}

\subsection{Lifted-graph vertex set}\label{app:detector-vertex}

For a candidate good cycle $gc = [c_0, \ldots, c_{L-1}]$ on
$\mathbf{m}$,
\[
   V_{\mathrm{lift}}(gc) = \bigl\{ (k, q, a) : k \in \mathrm{Fin}(L),\,
     q \in \mathrm{Fin}(n),\, a \in \mathrm{Fin}(m_q),\,
     a \ne c_k(q),\, c_k[q := a] \in T_{N_1}(gc) \bigr\}
\]
with projection $\pi(k, q, a) = c_k[q := a] \in T_{N_1}(gc)$.

\subsection{Edge taxonomy}\label{app:detector-edges}

For each step $k \in \mathrm{Fin}(L)$, let $\mathrm{mov}_k$ denote the
firing processor.  An edge $(k, q, a) \to (k', q', a')$ exists iff
$k' = k + 1 \pmod{L}$ and $\pi(k', q', a') = $ apply-move of
$\pi(k, q, a)$ at $\mathrm{mov}_k$ (under either the cycle's rule or a
$\detOf$-determined rule).  The edge type is
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*]
\item \emph{transport} if $|\mathrm{mov}_k - q| \ge 2$ (mod $n$);
\item \emph{c\_self}  if $\mathrm{mov}_k = q$;
\item \emph{c\_left}  if $\mathrm{mov}_k = q - 1$ (mod $n$);
\item \emph{c\_right} if $\mathrm{mov}_k = q + 1$ (mod $n$).
\end{itemize}
The four types partition the forward edges.  The audit of $4795$
forward edges across the corpus found zero edges in any
\emph{other} bucket; the partition is complete in the forward direction.

\subsection{LP formulation}\label{app:detector-lp}

\[
   \text{find } \Phi : E_{\mathrm{lift}} \to \mathbb{Q}_{\ge 0}
   \text{ such that } B^\top \Phi = 0 \text{ and }
   \textstyle\sum_{e} \Phi_e = 1,
\]
where $B$ is the (vertex $\times$ edge) incidence matrix of the
lifted-defect digraph.  The positive-mass normalization
$\sum_e \Phi_e = 1$ implements the $\Phi \ne 0$ condition as a
linear constraint suitable for a standard LP solver.

\textbf{Exactness.} The LP is rational-valued by construction ($B$
has $\{-1, 0, +1\}$ entries).  The reported implementation uses a
floating-point simplex for feasibility and then rationalises the
returned solution; feasibility verdicts are post-checked by an exact
incidence-balance pass (\texttt{exact\_check.py};
\cref{app:manifest}) that refuses to accept a feasible verdict
unless the rationalised $\Phi$ satisfies
$B^\top \Phi = 0$, $\sum_e \Phi_e = 1$, and nonnegativity
$\Phi_e \ge 0$ on every edge (zero-flow edges are allowed and
common).

Infeasibility verdicts on the reported corpus are shipped as
\emph{integer-valued topological-sort certificates}, which are the
Farkas witnesses for this LP form: for each of the $14$ LP-infeasible
records (four small-$n$ absorbers w5..w8, six CLB ternary-strip
$n \in \{5, \ldots, 10\}$, and the four $n = 9$ counterexamples of
\cref{sec:detector-n9-counterexamples}), the artifact
\texttt{farkas\_certificates.json} (\cref{app:manifest}) contains
an explicit vector
$y : V_{\mathrm{lift}} \to \mathbb{Z}_{\ge 0}$ (\texttt{topo\_order})
such that for every edge $(s, t) \in E_{\mathrm{lift}}$,
$y[t] > y[s]$.  Any non-zero non-negative circulation $\Phi$ would
satisfy
\[
   0 = y^\top (B \Phi) = \sum_{e \in E_{\mathrm{lift}}} \Phi_e \bigl(y[t(e)] - y[s(e)]\bigr)
     \ge \sum_e \Phi_e \cdot 1 > 0,
\]
a contradiction; equivalently, the lifted-defect digraph is a DAG
(topological sort exists iff no directed cycle).  A standalone
reviewer-runnable verifier (\texttt{farkas\_verify.py};
\cref{app:manifest}) takes the certificate JSON, rechecks $y[t]
> y[s]$ on every edge of every record, and exits
with a status equal to the number of violations (expected $0$).
The certificate is purely combinatorial, no LP solver, no
floating-point, no external dependencies.

\subsection{Hoffman correspondence}\label{app:detector-hoffman}

\begin{proposition}\label{prop:hoffman}
The LP of \cref{app:detector-lp} is feasible if and only if the
lifted-defect digraph contains a directed cycle.
\end{proposition}
\begin{proof}
This is Hoffman's circulation theorem~\cite{Hoffman1960}: a finite
digraph has a non-trivial non-negative circulation iff it has a
directed cycle.\qed
\end{proof}

\section{Exhaustive verification methodology}\label{app:exhaust}

The $\ge$ direction of \cref{thm:exact-values} at $n \in \{5,
\ldots, 9\}$ is discharged by this appendix: the pre-generated
rejection-certificate stream under \texttt{artifacts/rejections/}
in the release tag witnesses every sub-target product-class
elimination (\cref{app:exhaust-artifact}).

\subsection{Enumeration scheme}\label{app:exhaust-enum}

For each $n \in \{3, \ldots, 9\}$ and each target product
$T < \Mn$ (strictly below the claimed minimum):
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item enumerate every multiset $\mathbf{m}$ on $n$ positive integers
$\ge 2$ with $\prod m_i = T$;
\item for each multiset, enumerate cyclic orientations modulo cyclic
rotation, reflection, and (where applicable) state renaming;
\item for each oriented state-count vector, enumerate candidate good
cycles;
\item for each candidate good cycle, enumerate consistent partial rule
tables and full completions, applying the pruning rules of
\cref{app:exhaust-pruning};
\item for each surviving completion, run the verifier of
\cref{sec:lower-method}.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Pruning rules}\label{app:exhaust-pruning}

The search uses two proof-critical pruning rules, forced-neighbor
consistency and processor-orientation orbit reduction, plus
state-label-renaming canonicalization inside each candidate good
cycle.  A third rule, \emph{quasi-unidirectionality} (no $4+$
cyclically consecutive binary processors), is used only as an
\emph{optimization}: candidates pruned on this ground are also
emitted in a dedicated replay class and the final verifier of
\cref{sec:lower-method} rejects each of them independently.  The
lower-bound certificate of
\cref{thm:exact-values} therefore does not
depend on trusting quasi-unidirectionality as a lemma; it depends
only on the verifier and on the two proof-critical pruning rules.
Soundness proofs for the proof-critical rules are in
\cref{app:exhaust-sound}; \cref{lem:quasi-uni} (the stand-alone
quasi-unidirectionality statement) is recorded there for completeness
but is explicitly flagged as non-proof-critical.

\subsection{Soundness proofs of the pruning rules}\label{app:exhaust-sound}

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Quasi-unidirectionality (non-proof-critical).}  The
following statement holds in the six-property connected model of
\cref{sec:prelim-model} and is used by the search as an optimization
only (per \cref{app:exhaust-pruning}); the lower-bound certificate
of \cref{thm:exact-values} does not depend
on it, because the final
verifier of \cref{sec:lower-method} independently rejects every
candidate with four cyclically consecutive binary processors and
emits a replay-class rejection certificate.

\begin{lemma}[Quasi-unidirectionality, connected model; optimization only]\label{lem:quasi-uni}
Let $(\mathbf{m}, f_0, \ldots, f_{n-1})$ be valid in the sense of
\cref{sec:prelim-five} (six properties including connectedness).
Then $\mathbf{m}$ does not contain four cyclically consecutive binary
processors: there is no index $j$ with
$m_j = m_{j+1} = m_{j+2} = m_{j+3} = 2$ (indices mod $n$).
\end{lemma}

\Cref{lem:quasi-uni} is used in this paper \emph{only} as a search
optimization: candidates with four cyclically consecutive binary
processors are emitted in a dedicated replay-class and
independently rejected by the final verifier of
\cref{sec:lower-method}, so the lower-bound certificate of
\cref{thm:exact-values} does not depend
on the lemma.  We therefore
do not give a proof sketch here; the lemma is \emph{not}
proof-critical for any claim in this paper.
\item\label{enumi:forced-neighbor} \textbf{Forced-neighbor consistency
(proof-critical).}  Each $\detOf$ entry is uniquely determined by the
candidate good cycle (\cref{sec:prelim-objects}); inconsistency
falsifies the cycle.
\item\label{enumi:orbit-red} \textbf{Processor-orientation orbit reduction (proof-critical).}  The dihedral
action $D_n$ (cyclic rotation $+$ reflection) on processor positions
is a homomorphism of the six-property connected model: the image of
a valid system is again valid (a routine check on the definitions);
hence one representative per $D_n$-orbit suffices.
\item\label{enumi:state-label} \textbf{State-label renaming (proof-critical).}  Let
$\sigma = (\sigma_0, \ldots, \sigma_{n-1})$ with
$\sigma_i \in \mathrm{Sym}(\mathrm{Fin}(m_i))$ be a tuple of
per-processor state-label permutations.  $\sigma$ acts on
configurations and rule tables by
\[
   (\sigma \cdot c)(i) \;=\; \sigma_i(c(i)),
   \qquad
   (\sigma \cdot f_i)(a, b, c) \;=\; \sigma_i\bigl(f_i(\sigma_{i-1}^{-1}(a),
   \sigma_i^{-1}(b), \sigma_{i+1}^{-1}(c))\bigr),
\]
with indices modulo $n$.

\begin{lemma}[State-label renaming is validity-preserving]\label{lem:state-label-bijection}
The map $(\mathbf{m}, f) \mapsto (\mathbf{m}, \sigma \cdot f)$ is a
bijection on systems with the same state-count vector.  It carries
good configurations of $f$ to good configurations of $\sigma \cdot
f$ (under $c \mapsto \sigma \cdot c$), and preserves all six
validity properties of \cref{sec:prelim-five}.  In particular, if
the designer declares the good configurations of $\sigma \cdot f$
to be $\{\sigma \cdot c : c \text{ good under } f\}$, then
$(\mathbf{m}, f)$ is valid iff $(\mathbf{m}, \sigma \cdot f)$ is.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
The action on rule tables is defined so that
$\sigma \cdot (f_i(c_{i-1}, c_i, c_{i+1})) = (\sigma \cdot
f_i)(\sigma \cdot c)_{i-1,i,i+1}$; hence the privilege relation,
the unique-move relation, and the move-output relation all commute
with the action on configurations.  Each of the six properties is
expressed entirely in those terms, so it is preserved.  Invertibility
of the action (hence bijectivity) is immediate from the pointwise
invertibility of each $\sigma_i$.  Full details follow the same
template as \cref{enumi:orbit-red}; the complete
clause-by-clause check is in
\texttt{docs/state\_label\_renaming\_proof.md} in the release tag
(\cref{app:manifest}).
\end{proof}

State-label renamings are applied during candidate good-cycle
canonicalization and counted separately from processor orientations
(see \cref{app:exhaust-certificate}, item C2).
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Certificate format}\label{app:exhaust-certificate}

For a referee to confirm that the enumeration is complete, each run of
the search driver produces, alongside the rejection certificates, a
\emph{coverage manifest} consisting of the following five records.

\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(C\arabic*)]
\item \textbf{State-count coverage.}  The list of every sorted multiset
$\mathbf{m}$ on $n$ positive integers $\ge 2$ with $\prod m_i < \Mn$,
together with the enumeration loop bounds and a re-enumeration hash
that must match a re-derivation from the integer-product constraints
alone.
\item \textbf{Processor-orientation coverage.}  For each multiset,
the list of every dihedral orbit representative (cyclic rotation $+$
reflection, i.e.\ $D_n$-orbit) of an ordered assignment of the
multiset's values to processor positions; the orbit sizes sum to the
ordered-tuple count $n! / \prod (k_v!)$, where $k_v$ is the
multiplicity of value $v$.  Local state-label renaming (the separate
action permuting the labels inside each $\mathrm{Fin}(m_i)$) is
handled later during candidate-good-cycle canonicalization (C3) and
does \emph{not} enter the processor-orientation counts of Tables
\ref{tab:n9-7776} and \ref{tab:n9-strict-between}.
\item \textbf{Candidate good-cycle coverage.}  For each oriented
state-count vector, the list of candidate good cycles enumerated, with
the pruning-rule invocations recorded per candidate.
\item \textbf{Partial rule-table coverage.}  For each candidate good
cycle, the list of partial rule-table completions considered up to the
first violation or full confirmation.
\item \textbf{Pruning-rule soundness.}  A self-contained restatement of
each pruning rule together with a proof obligation discharged in the
paper's model (\cref{app:exhaust-sound}).
\end{enumerate}

Each rejected candidate additionally emits a \emph{rejection
certificate} consisting of: the state-count vector; the candidate good
cycle (as a list of configurations); the partial rule table
considered; and the specific violated property (with the witnessing
configuration or move sequence that exhibits the failure).  A
certificate is accepted by an external checker iff the checker can
independently reproduce the failing move sequence and verify that the
emitted violation corresponds to one of the six properties of
\cref{sec:prelim-five}.

\subsection{Independent verifier}\label{app:exhaust-independent}

A second code path re-implements the verifier of
\cref{sec:lower-method} from the published configuration-space
definition without sharing internal data structures.  It accepts the
emitted certificates as input and re-checks each violation;
discrepancies between the two checkers are flagged at run time.

\item full per-certificate stream for $n \in \{3, 4\}$

\item full per-certificate stream for the all-binary

\section{Detailed failed-approach descriptions}\label{app:landscape-detail}

This appendix expands \cref{sec:landscape}.  For each tested approach
we record the formal statement of the proposed invariant; the
pre-registered discrimination criterion (the empirical signal whose
presence would have promoted the route to a candidate proof); the
verdict of the probe; the specific obstruction that triggered the
verdict; and the data link.  Following the structure of the
project's lower-bound history document (listed in
\cref{app:manifest}, $\S\S1$--$10$), the catalog covers the pre-SK
case-split architecture, the SK pivot,
the five-route SK arc, the transport-lift / twist-calculus arc, the
peel-direct approach, the A1 target-injectivity approach, the
SK Morse--Hamming formulation, and the sequence of topological
probes surveyed in \cref{sec:landscape}.  Each entry's contents
follow the template of \cref{sec:landscape-framing}.

\subsection{Pre-SK case-split architecture}\label{app:lndsc-presk}

Architecture: case split on cycle type given $\ge 3$ binary and
sub-threshold product.  Six cases (safe processor, ZW cw=0, ZW cw>0,
sweep consecutive, sweep non-consecutive, odd winding) with cross-case
dispatchers.  Verdict: stalled at 2--4 sorries; mutual recursion in
the proof logic prevents a closed Lean proof; the architecture is
fundamentally case-split.  The $3$-consecutive-binary case at $n \ge 9$
is itself an open analytical problem (no known mechanism).

\subsection{SK pivot and the 10-edge skeleton}\label{app:lndsc-skpivot}

Architecture: prove that the binary-cube projection of $\SK(C)$ at
sub-threshold $n \ge 9$ contains a canonical $10$-edge skeleton.
Verdict: the $10$-edge form failed empirically at maximally-spread
binary placements; replaced by a $4$-pole form; the audit retiring the
quantitative $|\SK| \ge 2^{n-1}$ target made the skeleton machinery
no longer needed for the main argument.

\subsection{The five-route SK arc}\label{app:lndsc-fiveroute}

Five structurally distinct routes were attempted on the
$\SK(gc).\mathrm{Nonempty}$ target: direct sync-cascade (reproduces
the A1-injectivity wall at step level); orbit-level quotient
(collapses to A1); fiber-counting / fiber-budget (routes through A1);
read-2 aggregate ansatz (independent quantitative wall at $n \ge 8$
binary-dominated); and abstract cycle-decomposition attempts
(jointly: the obstruction lives in the $(C, \mu) \times \detOf$
interaction, not in either component alone).  The named conjecture
surviving the arc is the strict-injectivity sibling of
\cref{conj:tsided}; empirical support is substantial
(no violator on $\sim 16.9$M terminals across $26$ seeds in the
three-consecutive-binary regime) but no analytical proof is known.

\subsection{Transport-lift / twist-calculus arc}\label{app:lndsc-twist}

Transport-lift arc: the forest bound was refuted; a directed-cycle
survives in the tube digraph $D_{\mathrm{tube}}$; the tube-index
cocycle is multi-valued; and a structural ``minimum Case-C $= 6$''
observation holds across $n \ge 6$.  The twist-calculus package
(Dominant / Fold / Fusion regimes) was designed to force a closed
escape-free walk on $D_{\mathrm{tube}}$; empirically the escape rate
grows linearly from $0.36$ to $0.43$ as $n$ increases, eliminating
the forced-closure route.

\subsection{Peel-direct approach}\label{app:lndsc-peel}

Empirical floor excellent ($100\%$ peel-nonempty across $1898$
records; exactly one non-trivial SCC per record; minimum SCC size
grows with $n$).  Analytical wall: peel existence is a global
property; no uniform deterministic walk rule simulates it
($26\%$ residue on best-of-five rules tested); no local predicate
characterizes SCC membership (min accuracy $46.7\%$); Mathlib has no
partial-function cycle-existence lemma at this shape.  Realistic
a direct bounded case-split formalization appears possible but
would likely be large and brittle.

\subsection{A1 target-injectivity approach}\label{app:lndsc-a1}

Empirical floor: $6289$ data points, zero violators.  Caller audit
broken: under unique-privilege assembly, mover and non-mover at the
same context cannot be assembled.  Moore pigeonhole fails: orphans
live outside the cycle.  HKR Index Lemma: structural preconditions
absent (oriented manifold with boundary, rank-symmetry group action,
chromatic subdivision).  Literature search exhausts the off-the-shelf
options.

\subsection{SK Morse--Hamming arc}\label{app:lndsc-skmh}

Conjecture: $f \supseteq \detOf(C)$ self-stabilizing iff $f$ induces a
complete discrete Morse vector field on the complement of $C$ with
critical cells in $G(C)$.  Probe-level verdicts on ten invariants:
Betti numbers either always zero, ambient-dimension-driven, or
inverted via coverage scaling.  Honest verdict: the LB has a
combinatorial core that any topological invariant of the ambient or
complement spaces is either $(n, L)$-driven, coverage-driven (wrong
direction), or reducible to $\SK$ via sink iteration.

\subsection{Topological probes}\label{app:lndsc-topo}

A systematic probe campaign covered, in order, six groups of
topological-style invariants.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*]
\item \textbf{Ambient invariants.}  Four of four tested invariants
($\pi_1$, linking matrix, Lefschetz numbers, Cheeger constant) did
not discriminate sub-threshold from valid-witness records.
\item \textbf{Lifted-defect circulation route.}  A candidate
separator.  Empirically robust across the full corpus but analytically
blocked at the mover-equals-defect step
(\cref{sec:landscape-flow}); the separator was also used to produce
a permanent positive side-effect, a Lean threshold bug was fixed
by splitting the previously unprovable \texttt{peelTube\_nonempty}
into the small-$n$ and large-$n$ arms matching \cref{def:Bn}.
\item \textbf{Balance-identity hardening.}  The direction-covariant
$3$-type balance identity closed to within a $4.9\%$ leading-order
residual, short of the $1\%$ discrimination target.
\item \textbf{Decisive small-$n$ tests and arithmetic pivot.}
Both the $4$-type and the direction-covariant $3$-type balance
identities failed as separators; the arithmetic-inequality pivot
is decisive (best extracted feature is the threshold condition
itself).
\item \textbf{Restricted-class candidate.}  One restricted-class
candidate survived as a conjecture on good cycles with
$\mathrm{longest\_ter\_run} \le 1$: if no two cyclically consecutive
$m_i$ entries both equal $3$, the lifted circulation LP admits a
feasible flow supported on transport $+$ sided-context
($c_{\mathrm{left}}, c_{\mathrm{right}}$) edges alone, with no
$c_{\mathrm{self}}$ edges used.  The extended check
(\texttt{longest\_ter\_run\_restricted\_check.py}; \cref{app:manifest})
confirms this on all $58/58$ in-subclass records of the $n = 5$--$8$
sub-threshold corpus plus the $n = 9$ Table 7 stretch ($68$ records
total); the $4$ LP-infeasible records at $n = 9$ are precisely the
non-adjacent $\{2^3, 3^5, 4\}$ orientations, all of which lie outside
the subclass ($\mathrm{longest\_ter\_run} \in \{3, 4\}$).  Two further
probes were inconclusive.
\item \textbf{Nerve and non-standard sheaves.}  A proper \v{C}ech
$H^1$ computation via the nerve of the cover produced $\beta_1$ in
the range $[27, 11179]$ at valid-witness records, not zero; tube
refinements, transport-plus-sided-only circulation, Conley-index
$\beta_1 / |N|$ ranges, and three non-standard sheaf formulations
all failed to separate the corpus.
\end{enumerate}
Each group was retired on a pre-committed empirical or analytical
kill criterion.
